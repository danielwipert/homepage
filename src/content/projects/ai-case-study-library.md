## The problem

Almost everything written about AI in practice is either a vendor's success story or a cautionary anecdote. Neither tells you how strong the evidence is, or whether the AI actually caused the outcome being credited to it.

## What it is

The Applied AI Case Study Library is an open, evidence-first record of AI in practice. It covers law, work, safety, healthcare, public services, and infrastructure: from training pauses at OpenAI, to an NHS triage trial, to more than two thousand court rulings involving hallucinated filings.

## How it's made

I research and write every case myself, from primary sources only: court records, filings, official reports, the original studies. Each one is heavily vetted before it goes up.

Three rules hold across the library:

- **Every claim is traced** to the evidence behind it.
- **Every conclusion is graded,** A to C, on the strength of that evidence, and labeled for how confidently it can be said that AI caused what happened.
- **Every revision is tracked** in Git, so any change to a case is on the record.

The library is open to contributions.

## Why it matters

It's the evidence side of the [AI Operations Management](/work/aiom-textbook/) textbook. The book sets out how AI should be run; the library shows what happens when it is, and isn't.
