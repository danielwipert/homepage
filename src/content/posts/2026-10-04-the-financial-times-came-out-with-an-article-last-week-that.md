The Financial Times came out with an article last week that confirmed what most of us have expected. Open weight models are going to gobble up market share very fast, and they are never going away.

The Financial Times put out an article last week entitled "Corporate America embraces cheaper ‘open’ AI models." This is huge because it's the Financial Times. I am going to sound pompous, and I am, but FT is like the big brother to WSJ: better, smarter, and more serious.

The insights within the article are startling:

- During August and September, executive discussions surrounding 'open weight' and 'open source' AI models in earnings calls and investor events increased sixfold year-over-year.

- The dominance of open-weight models accelerated dramatically: on Vercel’s AI Gateway, their share of processed tokens climbed from 7% in December to 56% in August. OpenRouter mirrors this momentum, as models from Chinese labs claim the majority of top positions by token usage.

The story goes on to detail how Tinder has started to use open-weight models even for non-technical users. Rippling had a great piece in Tech Crunch back in August about this same move: model routing, cheap models first, then layering up to expensive models in parallel with the complexity of the task or its priority.

The needle drop is the fact about AT&T, which feels like it would turn slower than the Titanic. They are now using open weight for 40% of the AI workload and hoping to hit 70% by the end of 2026. That's insane, really, for an old dinosaur company.

The call most of us were making at the start of 2026 has come true. Open weights will be the workhorses of AI. Frontier will be like the Apple, top shelf, top cost. But my view has actually shifted a bit, pointing in a different direction than companies self-hosting and fine-tuning their own models.

I think there is a massive layer of open ground in between the model and the user. OpenAI and Anthropic have owned this space, but they have a major problem, which is the same problem Apple had with the smartphone. Their product is amazing, but it is a walled garden. Their selling point is their model, kinda like iOS or the superior UX of Apple.

The issue is that Gen AI is an open tech. It is probably the most open tech we have ever seen that is this powerful. Anyone can create in this space, especially with open-weight models pouring out of China on a daily basis. This means the model is never going to be the moat. The system and platform surrounding the model is the moat.

Just like with the web, everyone tried to do everything in the beginning, but then the market became layered, specialized, and refined. The same will happen in AI. The bigger winner I am seeing over the next year. The company that can create a truly brilliant, model-agnostic tool that anyone can use: from a specialist to a COO. Think TypingMind, but it's a million times easier to use. That's a billion-dollar company.
