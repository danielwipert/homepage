Tariffs are generating inflation - Financial Times 8/12/2025

The Pass-Through Has Started...

- Yale Budget Lab estimates the effective US tariff rate is currently 17.7 per cent. This would be the highest rate in a century. The tariff shock is large and still building.

- Macro rule of thumb implies a measurable price impact as new duties pass through. “we would expect prices to rise about 0.7 per cent”

- Who is paying? Mostly U.S. buyers. Import prices show the U.S. “paying,” with only limited foreign absorption; Goldman now estimates exporters eat ~14% (possibly up to 25% if early effects persist).

- Margin squeeze now, price hikes next. Firms report pressure on input and output prices (notably for intermediates like steel). The last time PMI price gauges were this elevated, inflation followed.

-- This is what the regional Fed reports have been showing as well. Increase of input costs, but not yet consumer prices, which is Margin Crush. It won't last, prices will go up and inflation increases --

- Policy angle: “With this data, the Fed could easily justify waiting longer before cutting rates,” while expectations “have risen… but have not moved much.”
