The rise of Z.ai snuck up on me. I have been using GLM-5.3 Flash recently, and I did not even realize how popular it had become. I like to try everything, and it is impressive for the price. But the bigger story is how Z.ai is starting to gain ground and ride in the shadow of DeepSeek.

First, for anyone not in the weeds, Z.ai (formerly known globally as Zhipu AI) is a major Beijing-headquartered generative AI company spun out of Tsinghua University. It is one of China's primary LLM developers. Listed on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange in early 2026, making it China's first major publicly traded pure-play LLM developer.

GLM-5.3-Flash is Z.ai's open-weight model focused on agentic workflows and coding. First appearing anonymously on OpenRouter under the codename Ox Alpha. Why is it winning?

- It’s very cheap -- about $0.05 per million input tokens and $0.16 per million output. That's definitely a budget play, but its power is nipping at frontier levels.

- It’s built for coding agents -- what is great about GLM is that the value combo is perfect for token-heavy coding agents. Agents typically generate thousands of invisible tokens running loop commands. So power users in Aider, Cline, Roo Code, etc are loving it.

GLM helped Z.ai jump to number 2 behind DeepSeek in Sept. Usage is holding steady in Oct as well. Z.ai has actually created more distance between 3rd (OpenAI) and 4th (Xiaomi) this month so far. It is the clear number 2 for right now.

But does anyone stand in the way of DeepSeek dominance? Even with the new ban from DHS following bans from the Commerce Department and the Navy, I don't see DeepSeek fading at all. With the big stories in NY Times, WSJ, and FT about the rise of open-weight models, it might push its token share up to 40 or 50% by the end of the year. The rest of the labs might be left with crumbs. At least for now, AI changes like wind.
