Reviewing V3 of my City Economic Health Report. The winner right now is Raleigh-Cary metro in North Carolina...

Unemployment & Labor Market

Raleigh’s unemployment rate of 3.0% remains below the 3.5% threshold, indicating a tight labor market. However, the 0.2 percentage point year-over-year increase points to slight softening. While conditions remain strong, the uptick suggests marginally weaker absorption or labor force growth outpacing job creation.

Workforce Supply

The labor force is expanding at 0.53% year-over-year, a modest pace that ranks in the 36th percentile. This gradual increase suggests limited near-term labor pressure, but could constrain future growth if hiring demand accelerates.

Wage Growth

Average hourly earnings are rising 5.28% year-over-year, comfortably above the 4% benchmark. With inflation near 3–4%, workers are seeing real income gains, supporting consumption and reflecting sustained demand for labor.

Labor Demand

The Labor Demand Composite score of 6.28 reflects solid conditions. Nonfarm employment is growing at 1.25% year-over-year, while hours worked are slightly above baseline (+0.23%). Employers appear to be extending hours before expanding headcount, indicating steady but measured demand.

Cost of Living

Raleigh’s cost of living ratio of 1.41 (75th percentile) signals moderate housing costs relative to earnings. The city remains more affordable than many peer markets, preserving purchasing power despite rising wages.

Housing — Construction

Building permits have surged 38.82% year-over-year, indicating strong construction activity and builder confidence. While this may help ease price pressures, the pace raises some risk of overbuilding if demand slows.

Housing — Market Velocity

Median days on market have risen to 46 days (+4.55% YoY), signaling a cooling housing market. While velocity remains relatively strong (Composite 7.42), the upward trend in time-to-sale indicates softening demand and increasing inventory.
