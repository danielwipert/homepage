Probably the most buzzed and hyped word of 2026 is Agent. Agentic AI started to take off as a concept just about one year ago. I remember learning about it in my Kellogg School of Management classes, but there was no real guidance. It was just an idea, a prediction really. Now it is what everyone is trying to build.

The Open Claw moment early this year helped propel Agentic AI forward, the idea that you could just give AI a task or group of tasks, and AI would do it. That lasted about a month tops before everyone realized what a security nightmare Open Claw had become. But the idea of Agents underpins the entire AI industry. Agents are the utopian promise that backs every cent being poured into AI right now. It is the dream being sold.

### The Agentic Industry

The problem with the term Agent is that no one has any clue what it actually means. If we had to define it as the industry uses it: *an AI system that you give a goal to, which plans its own steps, takes real actions with tools, and keeps going until the goal is met. *It sounds all very rosy. You need something done, like a board deck, you give the AI agent some instructions, and off to the races.

Here is the problem with all of this. The industry definition is delusional. The idea of an AI Agent is a cyberpunk fantasy that buys into the illusion that semantic computing is the same thing as reasoning, logic, and understanding. AI is not conscious, nor does it understand. It is math. But alas, there is something to the word Agent that we can actually use.

What we really mean by Agentic AI is AI that is goal-seeking and plans towards that goal automatically. It is just a higher level of automation that uses data, math, and natural language processing. It is not magic. Goal-seeking means working toward an outcome rather than completing one instruction. Planning means breaking the goal into sensible ordered steps. Autonomous means acting without approval at each step.

### Agents Are Systems, Not Models

The biggest breakdown in communication within the industry is mistaking the term "agent" for "model." Models are not agents. They can't be. A probabilistic token generator can never rise to the definition of an agent. Agency requires a lot more than an LLM can offer. In order to achieve the very lofty goal of an agent, you need a well-structured and regulated system.

Actual agents will come from an emerging field within AI called Compound AI Systems,* a system in which the language model is one component among many, and the reliability of the whole comes from the parts around the model, not from the model alone. *When I first started to undertake agentic AI, it became clear the whole model-to-agent evolution was impossible. Models are self-contained entities that are immensely powerful, but they are open loops. There is no self-regulated loop to read the environment, read its own output, and adjust its work accordingly.

Agency requires closed loops and feedback mechanisms. No single model will ever be able to achieve that. But a compound AI system can. That means a model is just a node in a complex system that could include more models, tools, stages, gates, logging, permissions, long-term memory, and much more. Compund AI Systems are the missing link between what we have now and Agentic AI.

### Can a chatbot be responsible?

So what is the difference between a chatbot and an agent, according to current industry standards? Let's say a customer wants a refund. An agent reads the refund request, checks the policy, issues the refund, and emails the customer. A chatbot would have told the customer how to request a refund and stopped. The agent owned the outcome. But the biggest issue here is that ownership does not presuppose responsibility. People can be responsible; AI can not.

The compound AI system version of this AI refund workflow might look like this: the refund policy was checked by a RAG step over the current policy documentation. The refund went through a tool with a spend cap. A second check flagged a duplicate request on the same order. Every step was written to a log. Anything over the cap, or any order with an open dispute, went to a human queue. That is an autonomous Agent. It is a system with checks, gates, and logs. Not just a model doing what you want it to do.

### Agent Washing

Because Agentic AI is so hot right now and the term Agent is not properly defined or fully understood, we are seeing a lot of "Agent Washing." This is when consultants and AI firms lie to their customers by slapping the word "agent" on top of chatbots, which are really just LLM API calls with a wrapper. Those are not agents. Those are simple LLM tools. The grift is that you can 10x the cost of an LLM API call by calling a chatbot an Agent.

The other side of this is the Underbuild. A team buys the simple definition, budgets for one worker, and ships the model alone with no surrounding system support. Over a weekend it approves several hundred refunds against a stale policy, with no cap, no duplicate check, and no log to reconstruct what happened. People expect miracles from AI because that is what is being sold by the word Agent.

An agent is not a smarter chatbot. It is a system with real authority, and most of the system is not the model. This is why there is a rise in terms like loop engineering and now harness engineering. Harness engineering is a baby step away from Compound AI Systems. The takeaway is simple. Models are not systems or agents. They are nodes within a system. A viable system that is safe, stable, and reliable has the potential to become Agentic AI.
