Here my results for City Economic Health for US Metros ranked 6 to 10 in population. I also added a scoring readme on GitHub so anyone can see exactly how I am scoring this currently. I think I am on v20 of the scoring system, and I have already found a few changes I want to make for v21.

Some quick thoughts on this grouping:

- Miami - Strong employment scores, but Labor Participation is low. This might be skewed due to high retired population, may tweak this scoring a bit to reflect. Housing is also dragging down its grade. Days on market is bad.

- Wash DC - Surprisingly strong labor health, but low earnings growth. Mixed picture on housing. While houses are still moving fast, there is a huge drop in price per sq foot.

- Atlanta - Tale of two cities. Very strong labor scores. Housing is weak and contracting.

- Philly - Strong labor engagement but low earnings growth. Very surprising high cost of living. This a rather elaborate calculation I do based on avg hourly earnings vs housing cost and then looking at growth and variability derivatives of that. In Philly, the cost of living (wages vs housing) is high, getting worse, and volatile.

- Phoenix - Decent labor engagement and earnings growth. Housing is dragging it down. Collapse in new building, selling price decreasing, days on market creeping up from metro average.
