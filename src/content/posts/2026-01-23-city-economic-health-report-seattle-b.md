City Economic Health Report: Seattle -- B

Economic data for the Seattle MSA points to a clear divergence between labor market strengths and housing market adjustments. The unemployment rate stands at 5.1 per cent, placing Seattle in the 8th percentile nationally and signalling weaker employment conditions than its reputation as a high-growth tech hub might suggest. However, this headline figure is offset by a labour force participation rate of 68.65 per cent, in the 86th percentile, indicating that a large share of the working-age population remains actively engaged in the labor market rather than sidelined.

Earnings growth reinforces this contrast. Year-over-year wage growth of 9.53%places Seattle in the 92nd percentile, pointing to acute competition for labor among employers. The combination of elevated unemployment and rapid wage growth suggests a labor market in transition: firms appear willing to pay up for specific skills even as overall hiring remains cautious, consistent with restructuring in technology and professional services rather than a broad-based contraction.

Housing indicators reflect a more pronounced cooling. Home prices declined 0.39 per cent year on year, placing Seattle in the 18th percentile and marking a clear shift away from the rapid appreciation seen earlier in the cycle. Residential construction activity has not collapsed, however. Building permits rose 2.65 per cent, a 62nd-percentile outcome, implying that developers continue to see medium-term demand, albeit with greater restraint. Homes are spending an average of 67 days on the market, close to the national midpoint at the 46th percentile, suggesting a market that is adjusting gradually rather than seizing up.

Overall, Seattle’s data describe an economy recalibrating rather than weakening outright. Strong participation and wage growth point to underlying labor market resilience, while softening house prices signal the unwinding of earlier excesses. The tension between these forces will be critical in determining whether the region stabilises at a lower growth rate or reaccelerates as hiring confidence returns.
