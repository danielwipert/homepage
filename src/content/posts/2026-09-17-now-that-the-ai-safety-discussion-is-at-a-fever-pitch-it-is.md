Now that the AI safety discussion is at a fever pitch, it is a good time to start refining the discourse. My specific viewpoint is not well represented, so I will state it here.

I am an AI pessimist who is not a doomer. I am an AI optimist who does not believe in singularity. I am a pessimist because I think AI is incredibly dangerous, but not because AI is rogue or evil. AI is dangerous because it is one of the most powerful technologies we have ever created. And no one besides the Luddites are taking that power seriously. On the other hand, AI is not actual intelligence, nor does it have understanding. AGI is a joke, and singularity is total lunacy. AI is math. It has no cognition or reasoning.

This New Yorker essay, I think, hits a bullseye on where I land in the doomer vs. utopianist debate:

"When A.I. is trustworthy, comprehensible, and predictable, it can be successfully integrated into business, government, defense, and personal life; if it can’t be trusted, it has to be kept at arm’s length. A.I. companies, therefore, will need their systems to become safe, because that’s where the money is."

Looking at last night's breaking news of more OpenAI breaches and hacks, I think it is clear that AI is not trustworthy in its current incarnation. How have we found ourselves in this position where Feral AI is running amok? To truly understand the origins of Feral AI, we need to go back to the original sin of Applied AI.

Since 2022, the center of the AI universe has been the model. This is the model-centric theory. It is wrong. AI is about systems, not models. We need our own Copernican Revolution in AI. We need to shift the center of Applied AI to the system; the model is just a planet in that system.

Until we make that shift, Feral AI will continue to create chaos, and AI growth will be stunted. Creating trust and safety in AI will require a paradigm shift. It is not about slowing down or banning models or government regulation. The shift will be ontological. Artificial intelligence is not just a trillion-parameter large language model. AI is a complex, well-engineered, closed-loop system that helps people and never harms them. Until we transcend to that level of craftsmanship and professionalism, AI is not reliable, safe, or trustworthy.
