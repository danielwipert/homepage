July 2025 Monthly Housing Market Trends Report  (Realtor.com)

Inventory of homes for sale rose 24.8% year over year...

- Demand–supply mechanics: buyer leverage up, transactions still throttled

- Inventory up 25% YoY; 21st straight monthly gain. Genuine easing of the post‑pandemic scarcity, albeit still below 2017–19 norms. More choice = less bidding‑war pressure = slower price growth.

- Mortgage rates still 6.6–6.8% (late July/early Aug). Rate levels keep many would‑be movers locked in, capping transaction volumes even as supply rises. Net effect = more listings sit longer, price cuts increase, but sales don’t pop off

- Prices → Inflation channel (CPI/PCE)

- List‑price appreciation near flat plus rising inventory points to softer home‑price momentum. That tempers shelter disinflation’s timeline, but is directionally helpful. Shelter is the heaviest CPI weigh.

- July CPI shelter rose just 0.2% MoM, with rent/OER up 0.3%. If resale prices keep cooling, CPI shelter should gradually ease with the usual lag.

- Wealth effect & consumer spend

- Cooling or flat home prices mean less housing‑wealth tailwind to discretionary spending. If homes aren’t selling and prices aren’t rising, people feel less wealthy and buy fewer big-ticket goods.

- Regional divergence, labor mobility & local growth

- Inventory surges and more frequent price declines are most visible in the South and West, while Northeast and Midwest stay tighter. Expect asymmetric local effects: cooling markets could drag down local economies, while tighter markets may keep growing.
