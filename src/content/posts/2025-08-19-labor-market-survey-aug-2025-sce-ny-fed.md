Labor Market Survey -- Aug 2025 (SCE, NY Fed)

Share of Those Still Employed After Four Months at Lowest Reading Since July 2020.

What is this report?

A recurring New York Fed survey that follows the same households and asks about job changes, job search and offers, pay, and expectations for the next four months. It’s forward-looking and tracks the same individuals over time, helping to spot early shifts in labor-market tightness, wage pressure, and worker satisfaction before they appear in official data.

-- Takeaways from Aug 25 Survey --

- Wage Satisfaction has tanked - The Aug data of 53.7 is the worst in the entire history of the survey, going back to March 2014. This is about two standard deviations away from the Mean of 58.4, so while it's not a good reading, it is more of a watch point than a true outlier. It is an early warning of a potential shift.

- Pay Expectations outrun offers - Workers’ minimum acceptable pay hit a record $82.5k, while the average offer slipped to $70.3k, a widened $12.2k gap (much wider than March). Implication: more declined offers and longer time-to-hire unless firms raise pay or candidates reset expectations.

- Churn is easing; pipelines are thinner - Job moves dipped to 6.6% and only 17.6% received an offer in the last four months. Workers also expect lower offers ahead ($66.6k). Implication: Poaching pressure is lighter, but hiring remains slower than last year

- Cooling shows up as unemployment, not dropouts - Transitions to unemployment rose to 3.3%, while exits from the labor force fell to 3.1%. Implication: Participation is holding up, but slack is creeping in, suggesting a gradual cooling of the labor market rather than a collapse.
