“There is a cheaper model that scores just as well” is not yet a reason to switch. A substitute model has to clear two bars...

First, it has to be good enough for the actual work.

Model adequacy is determined by the requirements of the task and the workflow around it, not by general capability. A leaderboard can tell you that DeepSeek Flash V4 performs well on a benchmark. It cannot tell you whether it will perform well enough inside your production system.

Second, the economics of switching have to work.

Model substitution is not free. A new model may require new evaluations, prompt changes, routing logic, integration work, regression testing, monitoring, and operational support. Those transition costs have to be weighed against the savings or performance gains that motivated the switch in the first place.

None of this means switching models is usually difficult or expensive. Sometimes substitution is nearly frictionless, and the economics are obvious.  
The point is narrower: A cheaper adequate model is a candidate for substitution, not yet a business case for substitution.

Capability clears one bar, but you shouldn't switch unless the new model creates excess value even with the sub costs. Switching ain't free.
