Empire State Manufacturing Survey For July just dropped. Some good news I think:

TLDR: New York’s manufacturing sector is showing renewed momentum, with business conditions turning positive and capital investment on the rise for the first time in months. Yet ongoing cost pressures and supply chain challenges mean the recovery remains uneven.

Good News: Signs of Rebound

- First positive reading since February: General business conditions index jumped 22 points to 5.5, signaling renewed activity.

- Employment improves: Both the number of employees (+9.2) and average workweek (+4.2) increased (first dual gain since 2022).

- Shipments and new orders up: Shipments hit a several-month high (11.5) and new orders finally turned positive (2.0).

- Capital spending outlook is strong: Forward-looking capex index surged to 9.2 from -7.3, indicating investment is returning.

Bad News: Inflation and Supply Chain Pressures Persist

- Input prices are rising faster: Prices paid index jumped to 56.0, the highest in months, cost pressures are heating up again.

- Supply availability worsened: Current supply index fell further to -11.0, while future expectations remained pessimistic

- Selling prices stable: Prices received held at 25.7, suggesting firms are eating some cost increases rather than passing them on.

Mixed Signals: Recovery is Tentative

- Unfilled orders still shrinking: Despite the uptick in orders, backlog is falling, a signal demand may not be broad-based.

- Future optimism is muted: While outlook index rose slightly (24.1), expectations for new orders and shipments actually dipped.

- Inventory build-up: Inventories rose sharply, which could reflect confidence — or slower-than-expected customer pull-through.
