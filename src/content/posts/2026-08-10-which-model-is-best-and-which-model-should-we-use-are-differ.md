"Which model is best" and "which model should we use" are different questions and different disciplines...

The "best model" question you can easily benchmark at Artificial Analysis. The "what model" question depends on what the actual task is, the exact workflow needed to be automated, and the expected outcome vs. the full cost to run the AI automation.

A model can sit atop the leaderboard and still be the wrong choice for the job in front of you. Not because the benchmark is a lie. Because model capability and right fit for the job are two completely different concepts.

This is what most companies who are deep into AI deployment are experiencing on a daily basis. And it is not looking great. The people in the weeds are seeing a very complicated process. There are hundreds of models to choose from and truly an infinite number of ways to create a harness or system around your model calls. Some things to think about:

- Start with the failure conditions.

Before comparing models, define what a successful output must contain, which errors are tolerable, and which failures require human review. “Accuracy” means little until it is tied to the consequences of being wrong.

- Evaluate the entire system, not the naked model.

Retrieval, context construction, tools, memory, validation, and escalation rules can matter as much as the underlying model. A smaller model inside a well-designed harness can outperform a frontier model dropped into a weak system design.

- Measure cost per successful outcome.

Token price is only one part of the equation. Include retries, latency, tool calls, human review, and the cost of errors. The cheapest model per token can be expensive if it takes three attempts; the best model can be wasteful if a smaller one completes the task reliably.

We are really just starting to build what AI will do in the workplace. The AGI lunacy and Model Derby are wastes of time. The real work starts where consequences are real.
