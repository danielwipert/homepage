I have been pondering the massive sea change happening with Applied AI, aka AI in the real world. On the one hand, we have Silicon Valley pumping out voluminous levels of noxious bull about how AI is going to take every job. Then you have the C-level suites gulping it down and taking absurd actions like promoting tokenmaxxing or culling their staff in an offering to the AI Gods.

I often take a lot of pleasure in calling out people on their BS. But in this case, the stupidity is hurting millions of people. It isn't funny, and it has gotten very old, very quickly. AI is now polling below ICE amongst Americans, just let that settle in.

At the same time, I am not some luddite doomer like Ed Zitron who claims generative AI is one big pyramid scheme. I think AI is insanely powerful. It is like the personal computer and internet rolled into one. Instead of happening over decades, it's happening in like 5 years.

But AI has gotten out over its skis. It has moved way too fast without establishing an actual ROI. How exactly can we tell if AI is really worth it?

Here is my take on a thesis for AI ROI:

"AI ROI is produced through a chain of economic conversion: capability must become useful work, useful work must improve a workflow, workflow improvement must create value, the organization must capture that value, and the captured value must exceed the full cost of adoption within a defined period and boundary."

Let me break this down:

- Capability must become useful work - An AI model only creates potential. It produces value only when it performs work that the business actually needs.

- Useful work must improve a workflow - Helping with an isolated task is not enough. That work must make the overall business process faster, cheaper, safer, or better.

- Workflow improvement must create value - A better workflow matters only if it produces something economically meaningful, such as lower costs, higher revenue, greater capacity, improved quality, or reduced risk.

- The organization must capture that value - Creating value is different from keeping it. Time saved or quality improved must be converted into measurable business outcomes.

- Captured value must exceed the full cost of adoption - ROI depends on total economics, not just access price. The captured value must outweigh implementation, integration, governance, operations, and ongoing usage costs.

- Within a defined period and boundary - Every ROI calculation depends on what costs, benefits, time horizon, and business scope are included. Change the boundary, and the ROI changes.

I think AI ROI is pretty straightforward, but it is not the story being told by SV or C-suites. This type of work is slow, hard, and complex. It is not flashy, and it doesn't pop stock prices. But AI will not move forward until ROI is firmly and consistently established.
