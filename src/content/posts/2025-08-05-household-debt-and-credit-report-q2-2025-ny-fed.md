Household Debt and Credit Report (Q2 2025, NY Fed)

Student Loan Crisis Resurfaces: 10.2% of Borrowers Now in Serious Delinquency

- Total household debt rose by $185 billion (1%) from Q1 to Q2 2025, reaching $18.39 trillion, as per the New York Fed’s Consumer Credit Panel

- Housing-related debt was the main driver, increasing by $131 billion to $12.94 trillion

- Non‑housing balances—including auto, credit card, HELOC, and student loans—rose by $45 billion (0.9%) in total, but...

The Student Loan crisis resurfaces

-- Serious (90+ day) delinquency rose notably for student loans, now at 10.2%, driven by the end of federal moratoriums and resumed reporting to credit bureaus --

- Student loan delinquency plummeted artificially during the relief period, now is spiking steeply due to resumed reporting.

- The current ~10% delinquency rate is still below the pre-pandemic estimate of ~14–15%, but trending quickly upward.

- Even before the pandemic, the Fed noted that delinquency was “understated” because millions were in deferment, forbearance, or grace periods, meaning they weren’t making payments but weren’t counted as delinquent either.

- When including those who weren’t paying for any reason, as many as 1 in 3 borrowers were struggling.

- Over 43 million Americans hold student debt.

- As of 2025, over $1.6 trillion is owed, nearly the size of Canada’s entire GDP.

- Over 10% of balances are now seriously delinquent (and rising), with millions more in forbearance or deferment.

We are seeing signs of systemic strain, not isolated borrower mismanagement.
