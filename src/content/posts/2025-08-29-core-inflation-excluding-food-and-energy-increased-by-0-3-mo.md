Core inflation, excluding food and energy, increased by 0.3% month-on-month and remains at 3.1% year-on-year. Why and what is coming...

- Shelter Costs: Shelter accounts for over 40% of the core CPI and rose 3.7% in the past year. This consistent upward pressure in housing costs is the largest single contributor to elevated core inflation. It's Housing Stupid!

- Sticky Services: Prices for medical care (+3.5% YoY), household furnishings and operations (+3.4% YoY), and motor vehicle insurance (+5.3% YoY) have all posted significant year-on-year gains. These “sticky” service categories tend to adjust prices less frequently but have seen steady increases recently. Tell me again the economy is doing great for the middle class and lower... its not.

- Tariff Impact: Recent tariff increases have begun to push up the cost of select imported goods and inputs, which can affect related service prices and keep underlying inflationary pressures persistent. Walmart announced prices increase, Caterpillar and GM are getting rekt by tariff costs.

- Declining but Still Present Goods Inflation: While goods inflation (especially in energy and some retail sectors) has eased, this relief is not enough to offset the categories above. The BLS notes core drivers like transportation services and medical care have increased by 0.8% in just one month.

Ok so what is gonna happen now? Where is inflation going? Powell said last week in his speech he believed inflation would be isolated. I don't get paid the big bucks, but this what I see:

- The average effective tariff rate in 2025 is 18.6%, the highest since 1933. Direct analysis shows short-run consumer price increases of about 1.8% across all goods, with especially severe hikes for clothing, shoes (as much as 39% higher short-term), and motor vehicles (+12.4%)

- Recent reports indicate that, “US consumers had absorbed 22% of tariff costs, but that share was expected to rise to 67% by October,” meaning a majority of new tariff costs are being passed directly onto consumer prices.

- The Federal Reserve Bank of Atlanta warns tariffs have created an “elevated risk of a widespread surge of inflation,” and companies facing these duties expect to “ratchet up year-ahead price increases” even more as import surcharges ripple through the supply chain.

You don't need a weatherman to know which way is blowing. Prices are going up, probably a lot, probably fast. Good luck with the rate cut in Sept.
