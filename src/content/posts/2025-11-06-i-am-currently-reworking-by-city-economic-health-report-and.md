I am currently reworking by City Economic Health report, and I came across this very interesting data on the current NYC employment downturn...

Again, I am not an economist nor did I study economics outside of a single class at University of Queen Mary in London. Despite what ChatGPT and Claude tells me, I am not an expert. But numbers are numbers, and they often tell some variation of the truth.

I was trying to revise how my report on city economic health calculates labor participation rate. It has been a challenging, but I did come across this lovely metric measured every month by U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics: "Average Weekly Hours." They measure this every month for most Metros in USA. Pretty amazing data.

Looking over the data for NYC, it is kind of mindblowing what is happening. Something has fundamentally changed with how many hours people are working each week in our biggest city. This metric is unique, because this reduction in employment will most likely not show up in unemployment numbers or even labor participation data. People are still employed, but getting or working less hours.

Here is what I am seeing. The US average of weekly hours worked has been remarkably stable over 20 years. The typical American worker has averaged 34.4 hours per week for the past 20 years. That's been incredibly stable:  through the 2008 financial crisis, through recoveries, through everything.

NYC's current 32.41 hours worked per week is over 9 standard deviations below the US historical mean. NYC workers are putting in almost 2 hours less than the national average. That's roughly 25% of a full workday missing every week.

Just some other red alerts from NYC data since August 2023:

- Every single month for the last 2 years has been below 33 hours
- Before August 2023, NYC was below 33 hours only 5% of the time
- Now it's 96% of the time (24 out of 25 months)

I am clearly not smart enough and educated enough to make sense of this. But what I know for sure is something has dramatically shifted in NYC employment in the last two years. The aggregate change seems small, but when compared to historical data, the drop in hours worked per week is just massive and suggests a structural change in how New Yorkers work.

Maybe someone out there with a PhD in economics or stats can qualify this more. What is going on in New York City?
