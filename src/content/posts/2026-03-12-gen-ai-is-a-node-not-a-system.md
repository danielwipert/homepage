Gen AI is a node, not a system...

In building several new AI-driven tools, I am becoming increasingly aware of a central fallacy in the current AI boom. The manic drive to apply AI to everything has blinded many of us, myself included.

While studying how to properly implement AI at Kellogg, I became fascinated with Agentic AI. It felt like the natural next step in the evolution of generative AI. That excitement is widely shared, and it’s where billions of dollars in investment are now flowing.

But I believe our current concept of Agentic AI is fundamentally flawed. We tend to assume that the agent and the model are the same thing. This assumption is one reason so much money and talent are being poured into the Model Wars: Opus vs. GPT vs. Qwen vs. MiniMax, and so on.

In my view, this is the wrong path. Agentic AI should be understood as a system, not a single model. Single-model agents will always be limited because they remain open-loop systems. No amount of self-checking or reasoning scaffolding can override the inherently probabilistic nature of a large language model.

For that reason, I do not believe a single model will ever truly achieve agentic status, despite the billions of dollars currently being wagered on that outcome.

Where I see Agentic AI becoming real is when generative AI functions as one node within a viable system. Here I find myself returning over and over to Stafford Beer’s concept of the Viable System Model.

If generative AI can operate within a larger architecture, one that includes layers of orchestration, verification, and governance, then we begin to approach something that could legitimately be called Agentic AI.

In this sense, Agentic AI will not be a single model or even a single agent. It will be a collection of interconnected viable systems, each performing bounded functions within a controlled structure.

That is where the true promise of artificial intelligence begins to emerge.
