Let's continue to talk about AI Value, the burning question most people don't want to face. AI has been a lot of fun. It feels like anything is possible; you can build whatever you want. But therein lies the problem. We are all over the place, building bridges to no place in particular. Then the value question pops up, and everyone scatters.

I already went in-depth on the AI Value question for software development. The answer right now is startlingly clear. Generative AI has dramatically increased value at the task level, but not the final output. The reason, as stated by two massive studies, is the verification layer, human reviewing. Just because you increased the amount of code 10x doesn't mean you increased the ability to review 10x.

With coding, AI Value add is all at the front end of the workflow. That might change over the next few years, but not in the immediate future. Moving on to another vertical, let's look at chatbots for sales and cx. What are we seeing with AI Value add there?

The paper "Generative AI and Sales Productivity: Field Experiments in Online Retail" (Fang, Yuan, Zhang, Donati & Sarvary 2026) is a good starting place. Between September 2023 and June 2024, researchers conducted seven randomized experiments on a major cross-border online retail platform involving sample sizes from roughly 30,000 to 13.7 million users or products. Each experiment held prices and other variables constant while comparing standard workflows with and without Generative AI.

They found that AI tends to be most valuable not as a replacement but rather as a way to fill gaps in sales and cx workflows. They saw a 16% increase in sales (actual AI Value, for once!) when AI was used to help people that were not getting any help at all. When AI was used as a counter to human workers or current tools, the gains were small, like 2-3%.

With customer service specifically, AI could not beat a human with tough cases. It was a split decision. But when AI assisted humans with those same cases, the end result was objectively better. Augmentation won over full replacement with a 11.9% increase in sales, again actual AI Value.

AI can be a net loss as well. One experiment looked at a general reasoning model replacing a human for Google AdWords management. It faceplanted big time by removing keywords people were actually searching for, which dropped clicks by 10%. AI Value can be negative.

So other fun tidbits from this study. The less active and lower-spending customers gained the most from their AI interactions, as their sales rose by 28% vs. only 7% for high spenders. Besides the Google AdWords debacle, there was no discernible loss in cx quality when AI was added to the workflows: returns and ratings held steady or improved.

This is proof that AI can add real value to a business. But it is not the big and bold YES that Silicon Valley is selling. It's a soft yes with some caveats. More on that coming soon...
