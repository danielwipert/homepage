The end of DTC...

I have to react to this one because DTC apparel has been at the core of my career. I was living in Portland, OR, not doing much when I came across Bonobos in the spring of 2010. I became enthralled with the idea of selling clothes online. I dropped everything and found a way to get a job working at Bonobos in NYC (thank you John Rote)

Helping build Bonobos was one of the most rewarding experiences of my career. It was the exact thing I wanted to do. And the reality is that we did it. We won. We were able to build a clothing brand that was 100% online. Sure, we added guide shops while I was there, but the core of business was always ecommerce.

That is what makes this sale of Everlane so sad to me. I have always been a true believer in DTC apparel. It is why I moved across the country to work for Bonobos; it is why I worked super hard at Summeralt as their director of ops and the same at Bodily.

A company should have a direct relationship with their customers. A company should be transparent, honest, and respectful of not only its customers but also its partners and its workers. That was the promise of DTC to me, a new way of doing business, a New Economy.

I know there is a new generation of entrepreneurs who are ready to start the next era of DTC apparel. But it is sad to see this first wave of DTC companies, crest and fall back into the relentless tide of megacorps. We should have done better.
