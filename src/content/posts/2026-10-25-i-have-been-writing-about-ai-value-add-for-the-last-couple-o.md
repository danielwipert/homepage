I have been writing about AI Value Add for the last couple of weeks. My stance is pretty clear. We have the tools to rebuild most business processes, but we have not yet done the very hard and long work of tearing down and rebuilding brick by brick. It will take many years of design and building to really see the benefit of AI.

Now this piece from Apollo's Chief Economist confirms some of my viewpoint. The post called "No Signs of AI Payoff Outside the Tech Sector" has made the rounds over the last 24 hours. Here are the highlights:

- Financial gains from AI are showing up inside Big Tech, not across corporate America. He compares Mag 7 vs. the rest of the S&P 500; only the 7 are showing an increase in revenue per employee. A little rough calc and indirect, but still a clear signal.

- The same pattern holds for profit margins. Mag 7 growing, the rest are flat. This is much more compelling to me. Large patterns across many different industries are telling, no increased margin with full AI integration? Not a good sign.

We could stop here. The argument for AI has always been: do more with less OR increase gap between income and costs. Neither has happened, Yet.

He goes on:

- Total-factor productivity has not increased. This is a slower, more backward-looking metric, but no lift means we haven't seen the productivity increase promised by AI. We are seeing a huge rise with coding, but other jobs, not so clear yet.

- AI spends now are both hyperconcentrated and often circular. The cash is not spreading through out the market; it seems almost like a feedback loop.

His final argument is that even cheap models won't raise the tide for all ships. Because the hard work is not using AI, it is implementing it in real-world business environments. I am 100% in agreement, and I think his argument mirrors mine, the same argument I have been making for over a year now.

Gen AI is a new jet engine. But we haven't invented the plane yet. Harness engineering is starting that process, but it is much too naive and shortsighted to fulfill the required mission. Think of all the systems in a 787. That is what we need to build. The models are just the engines, and we have so much work to do before AI can fly. Time to start building.
