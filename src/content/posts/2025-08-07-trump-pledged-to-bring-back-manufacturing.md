Trump Pledged to Bring Back Manufacturing. The Sector Is Sputtering. (WSJ)

Aggressive reshoring policy has failed to materlized...

- Manufacturing is shrinking, not growing

“From March to July, U.S. manufacturing activity contracted, according to the Institute for Supply Management’s monthly survey. The Manufacturing PMI last registered at 48, below the 50 score that differentiates growth and decline.”

- Tariffs are historically high but not very effective.

“The effective average tariff rate on all imported goods now stands at around roughly 18% versus 2.3% last year, the highest levels since the 1930s.” (This is honestly insane, and I would call it economic suicide. Any fool could tell you this is a terrible policy).

My note: Reshoring won't happen with these tariffs for two reasons. One, they are insanely high and make zero sense. Trump seems like he has a palm reader determining his trade policy. Patently absurd and stupid. Two, that is now how reshoring works. You have to have preventive measures and then a holistic and widespread set of many different solutions to solve offshoring. Tariffs are like slapping a band-aid on a broken leg.

- Uncertainty is stalling investment.

“He and others say the unpredictability of Trump’s trade wars still makes it impossible for companies to decide on big capital commitments, like moving a factory from Asia to the U.S.”

My note: The mortal enemy of all business is instability and uncertainty. The entire concept of a business requires you to understand and properly predict future outcomes. That's how you make money 🤣. Instability prevents this from happening. Trump has created more and more chaos, which is kryptonite for good business.

- Consumer demand is weak and affecting production.

Quote (Polaris CEO Mike Speetzen): “Consumers are really just reluctant to go spend right now unless they really need to or they’re fortunate enough to have the financial flexibility to do that.”

- Job losses and hiring slowdowns continue.

Quote: “The sector lost a total of about 26,000 jobs in May and June, and another preliminary estimate of 11,000 jobs in July.”

My note: We are seeing this in pretty much every economic report. You want another one. McDonalds says its breakfast business is struggling, because people no longer have jobs. Think about that for a second. This ain't smoke and mirrors. People have stopped buying Egg McMuffins. Should be an index really.
