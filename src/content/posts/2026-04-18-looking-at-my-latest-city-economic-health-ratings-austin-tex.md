Looking at my latest City Economic Health Ratings, Austin Texas is still doing very well despite the housing downturn happening there...

Austin has been an "it" city for a long time now. I would say since the late 1990s, Austin has been near the top of cool cities to move to in the USA. After nearly 30 years of booming, it does not actually feel like the growth is stopping all that much.

Unemployment is low. Overall Employment is growing, with weekly hours working starting to lessen. Wage growth is great at nearly 6% year over year. That is a very healthy sign of a stable work market. This is no longer a boom economy in Austin, it is a very strong growing market.

The most fascinating aspect of Austin's current economy is housing. Austin had a massive boom moment during covid. Housing costs went crazy. Housing costs went up over 60% in a two year period from 2020 to 2022. That is bonkers.

Housing has been coming back down to earth, but home costs are still 44% above what they were pre-covid. Also worth noting, building permits have plummeted nearly 30% year over year. So the boom may be over, but the growth is not ending, just slowing, less intense.

Here is the deal though. I have looked at cost of living in depth in Austin. You get paid more in Austin and cost of living is much much less than in the major cities, pretty much across the board.

What makes Austin so attractive from worker's perspective is this. You get paid well, objectively more than in huge cities like Chicago, Atlanta, Philadelphia, and the overall cost of living in Austin is just lower, even after the housing boom. That is why Austin is still growing so much. Until the housing and cost of living increases to parity with other major cities in America, it will keep growing.
