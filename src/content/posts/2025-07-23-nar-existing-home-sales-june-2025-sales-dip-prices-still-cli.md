NAR Existing-Home Sales - June 2025 - Sales Dip, Prices Still Climbing

- Sales declined 2.7% MoM, dropping to a 3.93 million annualized pace -- the lowest since Sept 2024
- Inventory eased by 0.6% to 1.53 million units, equating to about 4.7 months’ supply
- Median price hit a record $435,300, up 2% YoY and marking the 24th straight month of gains
- Mortgage rates remain around 6.7–7%, keeping affordability tight

---

- Stalled Sales Reflect Affordability Pressure

Elevated mortgage costs (~6.7%) continue to suppress buyer activity, with sales at a near nine-month low despite some seasonal momentum

- Supply Not Enough to Meet Demand

Although inventory ticked up annually (~16% YoY), it's still below pre-pandemic levels, and shrinking month-over-month, sustaining price pressure.

- Homeowner Wealth Building Continue

Rising prices are increasing household equity. NAR notes the average homeowner has gained $140,900 in wealth over five years. Great for Boomers who own 54% of homes, not so for Gen X and younger who have seen their rate of home ownership slide since 2008.

- Potential for Market Shift If Rates Ease

Chief Economist Yun points out a drop to 6% mortgage rates could unlock 160,000 new buyers, especially renters and first-timers.

- Regional Variability Signals Local Opportunities

Sales fell in the Northeast, Midwest, and South, but rose slightly in the West. Prices continue rising fastest in the Northeast and Midwest.
