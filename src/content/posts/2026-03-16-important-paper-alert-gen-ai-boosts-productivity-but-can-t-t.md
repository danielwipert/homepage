‼️Important Paper Alert‼️ Gen AI Boosts Productivity, But Can't Turn Novices Into Experts (Stanford and Harvard Business School)

I think this study will probably been seen as a no brainer to anyone in white collar management. If you ever had the pleasure of building complex systems while also managing people, the fallacy of Gen AI running the show is obvious.

Gen AI is augmenting work every single minute, right now. If you aren't using Gen AI with most tasks, you are way behind the curve. But at the same time, Gen AI is incapable of many important functions and responsibilities of the average office worker.

This study confirms that while AI can drastically reduce the time to train and help plan and plot your work, AI can not replace your expertise, judgement, and mental prowess. Here are the specifics:

The experimental design involved 78 employees divided into three occupational groups: 12 web analysts (insiders), 26 marketing specialists (adjacent outsiders), and 40 technology specialists (distant outsiders). Participants were tasked with creating web article conceptualizations and executing full articles on financial trading topics, with randomized access to bespoke GenAI tools fine-tuned specifically for these tasks.

The findings reveal that GenAI significantly reduces performance divides between occupational groups, particularly for conceptualization tasks. Both marketing specialists (adjacent outsiders) and technology specialists (distant outsiders) were able to match web analyst performance in article conceptualization when using GenAI.

However, for article execution—which requires more embodied, context-sensitive knowledge—only marketing specialists could bridge the gap with web analysts. Technology specialists, despite using the same GenAI tools, could not achieve equivalent performance in execution tasks

This divergence is explained by the concept of 'knowledge distance', the dissimilarity between skill demands of different occupations. The study introduces the 'GenAI Wall Effect', the point at which GenAI can no longer meaningfully reduce expertise gaps between insiders and outsiders. This wall emerges from the interaction between knowledge distance and task characteristics.

The research challenges traditional ideas about expertise and the division of labor. GenAI shifts the source of performance from narrow task repetition toward broader foundational knowledge, enabling work to move more fluidly across roles, while preserving the importance of deep expertise for problems beyond the “GenAI wall.”
