I missed this one because I don't work in recruiting, but I think IBM's shift to rehire Entry Level people is leading the upcoming curve in hiring...

We all have heard the stories: AI is taking every job, entry level people are doomed. Some of that is true, but the reality is much more nuanced of course.

IBM is pushing back against the extreme ideologies of OpenAI and Anthropic. No, AI will not replace all jobs. No, AI can not do all human work.

- While fears about AI-driven job losses persist, The Budget Lab at Yale recently found that concerns about AI displacing today’s workforce—including entry-level workers—remain “largely speculative,” suggesting that it’s too soon to know how disruptive it will be.

- “Entry-level roles are shifting from purely task-driven work to analysis, problem-solving and responsible AI use,” said Natasha Pillay-Bemath, IBM’s VP of Global Talent Acquisition and Executive Search

- “AI can boost productivity, but it can’t develop the next generation of technical leaders or innovators.” IBM Chief Human Resources Officer Nickle LaMoreaux added that the companies that will be most successful in the next few years are “investing in talent now to set themselves up for the future.”

- "If we don’t continue to invest in entry-level hires, what happens in 3–5 years?” she asked. “There’s no pipeline; the well simply dries up.”

As someone who has been neck deep in AI for the last year, I can tell you with high confidence, the immediate power and impact of Gen AI is being vastly overstated. It is revolutionary, but that revolution will take years.

Dumping people now because you think "AI can do it" is a move for non serious people who do not belong in business management. It also demonstrates the complete lack of understanding of how Gen AI actually functions.

The thing is, the actual people who are going to get left behind are the business managers and leaders who do not educate themselves on AI, ironically. A 22 year old who understands how LLMs actually work might be worth more than 35 year old with an MBA but doesn't know LLMs. This will mimic the digital and internet revolutions. The dinosaurs go extinct and the new flock took over. Age and experience will not be the delimiter, it will be curiosity and the desire to learn new things. The lean back mgmt culture is going bye bye.
