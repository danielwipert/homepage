Dario's post today is a step in the right direction, but I think the real threat of AI is not what he is claiming...

"I have become convinced that fully addressing the risks requires even more prudence — not just investing in risk prevention, but pacing the rate of capabilities advancement so that risk prevention has time to keep up. We must slow the pace at which we improve the capabilities of AI models."

Dario makes some solid claims in his essay. First, safety can not always be downstream from capabilities. Two, actions to increase AI safety need to be universal; every company and country needs to be on board. Three, AI safety is increasingly an operations discipline. Errors were made by people, not AI. This last part is where I most agree, and I take it way farther than him.

But I think he is missing the plot as well. He claims to want to slow down the pace, but he does not commit Anthropic to slowing down. He only commits to greater transparency. He also only wants to slow down if his competitors slow down as well. Perhaps most egregious, he promotes a regulatory moat to be built around the frontier labs in the USA. Only they could follow all the regulations he is promoting, thus leaving all the startups and innovators to die on the vine. He wants the fox to design the hen house.

Amodei argues that the US should restrict China’s chips, compute access, distillation, and ability to acquire model weights. The objective is to widen the American lead enough that US companies can slow down without being overtaken. China has demonstrated that frontier-adjacent intelligence can become cheaper, more open, and less dependent on American API companies. Amodei responds by proposing a governance system that would make the American closed-model oligopoly safer, stronger, and harder to challenge.

But beyond the corpo feudalist double speak, Dario is missing a key point. AI does not harm people, just like a gun doesn't shoot people. Someone pulls the trigger. He is comically mute when it comes to responsibility. He makes all these claims about how AI is becoming too strong and it is improving itself. But computers have been doing this for many decades. Are computers a grave threat to humanity because they can do math better and faster than us?

AI can process language, images, and sound faster than any human being could in a lifetime. That is extreme power. But we built it. We designed it. We released it into the world. The Hugging Face attack is not the paradigm of rogue AI. It is the paradigm of human hubris and gross negligence.

Slowing down AI is not about slowing down model development. It is about finally accepting responsibility for our own creation. The doomerism and utopianism of AI both do the same thing. They dissociate the collective and individual responsibilities implicit with using AI. Just because you are using AI doesn't negate ethics, morals, and laws. Time to grow up.
