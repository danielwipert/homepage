The debate is over, for now at least. The Financial Times article from last week laid it out pretty clear. Enterprise has fully embraced open weight models. When you think about AT&T, three words come to mind: massive, bureaucratic, and regulated. It is a classic utilities company. Well, they are currently using open-weight for 40% of their AI and plan to push that to 70% by the end of the year.

Think back to the start of 2026. Open-weight Chinese models were certainly in the air, but they were not seen as major contenders to OpenAI, Google, and Anthropic. Fast forward to today, and open-weight has completely dominated the marketplace, at least in usage. Spend is still being gobbled up by the big three. But usage has all shifted to open-weight.

I made the chart below to show how this massive and sudden change occurred. Open-weight crossed 50% of usage in April and now is steady around 70-75% of usage. Staggering change from the start of the year.

This is why enterprise is diving headfirst into open-weight models. They are good; they are cheap. The value is just way better than locking your business into a closed ecosystem with the big three.

I honestly don't know what this means for the AI industry. Anthropic is IPOing next month. If I could offer you a comparable product for a fraction of the price, why would you use Claude? That's the big question right now. What true advantage and moat do the Big 3 have?

I think the market will mimic the Apple vs Android bifurcation in smartphones. The Big 3 will dominate the wall garden of high-end AI. Open-weight will control the rest, which is most of the market. But it is going to be a chaotic mess for the next few years

But who is going to control the open-weight market? Why have American companies ignored it, besides Meta? How does the arrival of Jev change the game?

As the AI market matures, I am seeing insane growth in the open-weight marketplace with big moves in the small and medium model tranches as compound AI systems begin to dominate builds. You don't need five V8 engines to run a car.
