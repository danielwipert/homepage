The most common issue I see today is business leaders having no idea where or how to use Gen AI in their workflows. The answer I give is pretty simple...

A business problem becomes an AI opportunity only when the relevant task has borders, has usable information, has a clear standard of success, and has meaningful business impact.

- Has Borders

The task needs a clear beginning and end. Its inputs, expected output, and place within the workflow should be identifiable. “Improve reporting” is too vague. “Summarize monthly sales calls into recurring themes and risks” is concrete enough to test.

- Has the Facts

AI can interpret language, compare cases, identify patterns, and synthesize context, but only when the relevant information exists and can be accessed.  
When the necessary knowledge is scattered, outdated, or undocumented, the first problem may be information management rather than AI adoption.

- Has a Scorecard

Leaders need a recognizable way to judge whether the output is accurate, useful, and safe enough for the intended workflow. Without a clear definition of good performance, a pilot may produce activity without producing evidence.

- Moves the Needle

Improving the task should affect something the business values, such as cost, cycle time, capacity, quality, risk, revenue, or customer experience. A technically impressive result is not important unless it changes a meaningful business outcome.

The question of which AI model or platform should come last. Begin with the friction, isolate the task, determine whether these four conditions are present, and only then decide which AI system is appropriate. Start with the work, then think about how AI might be able to help. Don't shove AI into your work.
