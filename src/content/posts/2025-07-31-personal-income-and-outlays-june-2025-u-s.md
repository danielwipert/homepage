Personal Income and Outlays, June 2025 (U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis)

June 2025 report shows a broad-based, modest rebound in income and spending after May’s declines. Inflation, as measured by the PCE price index, is rising at a steady pace, while real income growth remains subdued. The personal saving rate has stabilized, showing no major shift in consumer behavior.

- Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE) increased $69.9 billion (0.3 percent). The increase in current-dollar personal income in June primarily reflected increases in government social benefits to persons and in compensation.

Inflation: Headline PCE and Core PCE (no food and energy) both increased 0.3 percent. Headline PCE is up 2.6% MoM, Core PCE is up 2.8% YoY. This is above the Fed's target of 2% inflation

My Note: The inflation rate here is elevated. Combine that with the Prices Paid vs Prices Received gaps in the Fed Regional reports:

- Firms are currently absorbing cost increases

They're facing higher input inflation but not yet passing those costs fully onto consumers. This creates margin compression, especially in competitive sectors like retail, services, and food.

- Future consumer inflation pressure is building

If input costs stay elevated, firms will eventually be forced to raise prices, leading to: A) Higher Prices Received b) Upward pressure on PCE inflation

This is the classic cost-push inflation setup: rising producer costs → delayed pass-through → eventual consumer inflation. If Prices Paid continues to outpace Prices Received, a reacceleration in consumer inflation becomes likely, especially if demand holds up
