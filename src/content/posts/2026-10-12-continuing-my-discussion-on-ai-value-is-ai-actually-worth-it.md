Continuing my discussion on AI Value: is AI actually worth it? Here are the papers that I was using for my last few posts:

Writing Code vs. Shipping Code: Productivity Effects Across Generations of AI Coding Tools - https://lnkd.in/geGAEQuU

Artificial Intelligence in the Firm: Bottlenecks in Software Production - https://lnkd.in/g6t8Jb_u

Generative AI and Sales Productivity: Field Experiments in Online Retail - https://lnkd.in/gfWnTP2h

If you are serious about AI Value, I think checking out those papers is a good start. Now moving on with the discussion of AI Value, let's talk about HR. This paper specifically: Voice AI in Firms: A Natural Field Experiment on Automated Job Interviews (arXiv 2607.28222 10 September 2026)

Here was the setup: Roughly 70,000 customer service applicants at PSG Global Solutions were assigned a first interview with a human, an AI voice agent, or given a choice. All interviews followed identical guidelines. Ultimately, human recruiters scored every session and made all final hiring decisions, meaning the AI simply gathered data, while humans passed judgment.

They found that AI interviews not only sped up recruiting but also drove real business outcomes. AI boosted job offers by 12% and increased both start rates and long-term retention by 18%, all while maintaining identical candidate productivity.

The AI succeeds through "controlled variance." When hundreds of recruiters conduct thousands of interviews, their differing styles create noise that clouds hiring decisions.

The AI eliminates this noise by standardizing questions and consistently covering key topics, while still adapting to each candidate. This defines where AI adds the most value: executing high-volume tasks where human inconsistency gets in the way of finding the best talent.

Human review didn't erase the AI's benefits because the AI improved information quality, not volume. This shows that when AI provides decision-makers with better inputs, rather than just more work, the performance gains survive human oversight.

This study shows that AI can excel at information gathering when criteria are strict and human variation creates noise. Despite finding the experience less natural, candidates overwhelmingly opted for the AI and reported normal satisfaction rates.

So one mark in the AI adds avalue column. But does it actually offset the cost to implement and manage?
