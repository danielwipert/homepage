We got the final April Customer Sentiment reading from U of Michigan yesterday. Big yikes...

The latest reading from the University of Michigan is a weak signal, not just because it declined, but because of where it now sits. At 49.8, sentiment has dropped back into levels historically associated with economic stress, typically seen during inflation shocks or just ahead of broader slowdowns.

What stands out is the composition: both current conditions and expectations fell sharply, with expectations down 7% in a single month. That matters because expectations drive behavior. When households begin to lose confidence in the future, spending tends to pull back, and that shift often shows up in the real economy with a lag.

More concerning is the simultaneous rise in inflation expectations, with one-year expectations jumping to 4.7%. This creates a difficult dynamic for the Federal Reserve: confidence is falling, but inflation psychology is rising.

That combination (weak sentiment alongside elevated inflation expectations) has historically signaled a more fragile consumer backdrop than headline economic data suggests. The labor market may still look stable on paper, but beneath the surface, households are becoming more cautious, which is often where broader slowdowns begin.

Also to note, we got a new stockholder breakdown yesterday of Customer Sentiment. The call out here: People who don't own stocks, they are EXTREMELY pessimistic right now. They started measuring these sub groups back in 1999, every single month. Even in the depths of Great Recession, the sentiment amongst non-stockholders never got this low.

The lowest before was 55 in June 2008. We have been below that previous low every single month since September 2025.

Also to note, the top third of stockholders, they think things are fine. They were more upset in 2022. Talk about a disconnect.
