I just got finished learning about Mistral's new models and then Meta's Muse Spark Open comes along. It feels impossible to keep up at this point, but it is certainly fun trying to surf all the waves of information coming at us. MSO feels like a big one, though.

I think the most immediate reaction I have to Muse Spark Open is that it confirms what many of us have been saying for a good year now. Open weight is going to own most of the AI market. The fact that Meta is releasing an 800 billion parameter GPT-4 class model to counter OpenAI and Anthropic, like WTF. Finally, American companies are investing fully in open weight. Hiding weights behind closed doors is so 2025, it's over.

Meta is being very shrewd here. They are releasing this model partly to destroy the valuations of the Big 3. OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google are sucking up billions in API fees. Meta is saying, hey, you can have this for free instead. The Chinese labs doing this obviously have a more political angle in expanding China's soft power throughout the world. But the idea is the same as Muse. Take down the Big 3 and make it a truly open marketplace for foundational models.

Muse Spark Open could shift some of the economics of AI from operational expenses to capital expenses. A company can buy the hardware once (Mac Studio setup) and run infinite inference with very low marginal cost. For high-volume agentic workflows, this could save millions of dollars. Also, there is the data privacy play here, like Mistral's Soverign AI concept. If your data must be private and sit within your org's borders, Muse gives you the option to do this and get high horsepower AI.

This all sounds very exciting. But you know what I am not seeing. I am not seeing a repeat of 1999 when every company had a dusty room filled with servers to run their website. I don't see companies going out and buying a hundred Mac Studios to run their AI. The local hosting play here seems flaccid. I don't see it. I did 6 months ago, but not any more.

What I am seeing is the AI market opening up. I have no doubt OpenAI and Anthropic will be pillars of AI in 5 years. But they will not be the king and queen any more, like they are now. Their current flaw is chaining the intelligence layer (model) to the execution layer (inference). That lock is starting to get very rusty, very quickly.

For years, the big AI labs ran a spectacularly lucrative racket: if you wanted their brainpower, you had to rent their server room at whatever tollbooth rate they pulled out of thin air. Ripping the model’s weights away from their cloud hosting blows up the whole casino.

Suddenly, the intelligence isn't a proprietary black box anymore. It is a free blueprint, and the tech giants are getting reduced to what they feared most: glorified, interchangeable plumbers forced to bid down the cost of raw compute to the penny. That is one major reason why Meta is releasing Muse Spark, to make sure their competitors lose pace and lose ground.
