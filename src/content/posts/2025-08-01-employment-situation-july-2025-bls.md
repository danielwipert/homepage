Employment Situation - July 2025 (BLS)

The Labor Market Stalls Amid Slowing Momentum...

- Only 73,000 jobs were added, a stark slowdown from earlier in the year and the weakest three-month average since the 2020 pandemic shock. May and June’s job gains were revised down by a combined 258,000, revealing that the summer slowdown was even worse than we thought.

-- “Total nonfarm payroll employment changed little in July (+73,000) and has shown little change since April.” (Page 1)

-- “The change in total nonfarm payroll employment for May was revised down by 125,000, from +144,000 to +19,000, and the change for June was revised down by 133,000, from +147,000 to +14,000. With these revisions, employment in May and June combined is 258,000 lower than previously reported.” (Page 3)

- Unemployment stayed at 4.2%, but the number of long-term unemployed rose sharply. Labor force participation ticked down again. More Americans want work but aren't actively looking—a sign of mounting discouragement.

-- “Both the unemployment rate, at 4.2 percent, and the number of unemployed people, at 7.2 million, changed little in July. The unemployment rate has remained in a narrow range of 4.0 percent to 4.2 percent since May 2024.” (Page 1)

-- “The number of people not in the labor force who currently want a job changed little in July at 6.2 million but was up by 568,000 over the year.” (Page 2)

- Healthcare remains the exception, adding 55,000 jobs, outpacing its own strong trend. Social assistance continued modest growth. But major sectors like manufacturing, retail, and professional services are effectively frozen.

-- “Employment showed little change over the month in other major industries, including mining, quarrying, and oil and gas extraction; construction; manufacturing; wholesale trade; retail trade; transportation and warehousing; information; financial activities; professional and business services; leisure and hospitality; and other services.” (Page 2)

Federal Jobs are vanishing, down 84,000 since January.

-- “Federal government employment continued to decline in July (-12,000) and is down by 84,000 since reaching a peak in January.” (Page 2)
