This story is precisely why I wrote my paper on AI Operations Management. AI is an operating cost, not a software cost...

- "Rippling was on track to burn 40% of its R&D headcount budget on AI tokens, meaning it was spending as much on tokens as 40% of all the compensation it paid employees in that unit. Millions of dollars."

- "Spending was growing by 80% month-over-month, and if that trend continued, the next year it would spend almost as much on AI tokens — 90% — as it spent on its high-paid R&D unit employees."

- That was a common early-2026 problem. Now, eight months into the year, enterprises have figured out a couple of things. First, they know they need multiple models from multiple AI labs at various price points, including a frontier open weight option, perhaps of Chinese origin.

This is all very predictable, and AI will require a whole new discipline within business management. AI is not a software cost. It is an operating expense that grows the more you use it, AND that growth varies based on the complexity of the request. It needs to be treated like part of the supply chain.

This means a whole theory of AI procurement. Then you need to figure out measurement, which is what Rippling is doing here. Then you need to work on attribution. After a lot more work, you need to start approaching the value question, the actual ROI. That is THE question.

Like I said, this is a whole new discipline. Here is my first draft on how I think it should work: https://lnkd.in/gK6Cy2-C
