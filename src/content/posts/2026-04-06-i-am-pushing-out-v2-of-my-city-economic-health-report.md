I am pushing out v2 of my City Economic Health Report. This measures the economic health of the top 50 metros in the United States...

I am calling this version 2, but it is really like version 50. I have been constantly updating and tweaking the data and python pipeline of this application. The biggest changes for this version are the scoring system and presentation.

Scoring has been drastically overhauled. I disliked the clustering of metros in the B range, so I made this more traditional A-F spread. Now, I already have a few ideas for version 3, namely long term historicals for each metro. But this is a massive leap forward from where this was when I first launched it. The scoring is not perfect, but the scores absolutely capture where each metros lands amongst the top 50 cities in the USA. Raleigh NC is objectively flourishing where Sacramento CA is definitely not.

The presentation has been rebuilt from the ground up. I admittedly do not have a lot of graphic design formal training. I have needed to dabble in it with every job I have had, but I am not naturally talented as a designer. But I am proud of the new look here. The report is live on the web, and you can download the PDF full report which includes the detailed score for every city and short analysis. It looks solid. Way better than it did a few months ago.

So please check it out, let me know your thoughts. I actually love disagreement and pushback, let me know if you think the scoring is flawed or you feel like I am skewing the data here. I am not data science or economics PhD. If you are, call me out where I am wrong.

Congrats to Raleigh-Cary North Carolina for being the clear number one metro area right now. This will change every week with new data coming in, but they are the king right now.
