Philly Fed Manufacturing Survey - August 2025

Prices Paid vs Prices Received gap increases indicating inflation risk...

Diving into these regional reports is always a great pulse read on what is actually happening throughout the country in different business sectors. These are surveys of actual business people running businesses, not talking heads. The newest Philly Fed report dropped yesterday, and there continues to be a clear signal that businesses are paying more for inputs while not fully passing these costs to consumers.

In short, input costs are rising faster than selling prices. One of the idiosyncrasies of these surveys is that this gap pretty much always skews to input costs. Over the last 15 years in this survey, the gap has been higher on the input cost side 92% of the months, the average gap being around 13 percentage points. This makes sense because costs have a much higher plasticity than selling prices.

But the input vs sell price gap is starting to increase rapidly, which indicates that companies are sacrificing margins to maintain sales volume. The current gap between Prices Paid vs Prices Received is at 31 points vs 24 points last month vs a 15-year avg of 13 points in the Philly Survey.

Is there a one-one correlation between this gap and goods inflation? Not really. But the Philly MBO survey is a directional signal. There is clear overlap with increases in goods CPI/PCE in the months after high Prices Paid vs Prices Received gaps. Its not uniform or bound, but it signals the general direction of the wind. Prices for consumers will be going up. They will be increasing higher than they are now. The velocity will be accelerating.

Powell is speaking as I write this. He is signally a rate cut in Sept, because he feels the price increases, which are all but certain now, will be short lived. I am not an expert or economics PhD. But signals like this cost-price gap suggest that price increases are coming and there is an inherent "inflation interia." That is, once a company or industry raises their prices, they become more comfortable with raising them again and again, looking at you Summer of 2022.
