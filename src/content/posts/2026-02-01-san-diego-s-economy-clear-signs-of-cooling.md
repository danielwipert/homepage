San Diego’s Economy: Clear Signs of Cooling 📉

Economic indicators for the San Diego–Chula Vista–Carlsbad metropolitan area point to an economy losing momentum at the margin and core.  
The unemployment rate stands at 4.6%, placing the region in the 24th percentile nationally, a respectable figure, but no longer a source of strength.

Labor force participation, at 62.4%, is materially below the national metro average of 64.6%, underscoring a structural weakness in workforce engagement rather than a temporary fluctuation.

More telling is wage growth. Earnings rose just 3.35% year over year, ranking in the 30th percentile, and pointing to muted income gains rather than a tightening labour market. 💼

Housing and Construction: The Drag Is Now Visible 🏠

The housing data is unambiguously softer. Building permits have declined 25.87% year over year, placing San Diego in the 12th percentile and signalling a pronounced pullback in new development. House prices have edged down 0.55%, underperforming the national trend.

Days on market remain relatively low at 57, well below the national metro average of 66, indicating that liquidity has not evaporated. Price softness appears to reflect a recalibration of valuations rather than a collapse in buyer interest.

Bottom Line 📌

San Diego’s economic performance is increasingly constrained by its elevated cost of living. The region now ranks as the fourth-highest cost-of-living metro among the top 50 U.S. metropolitan areas, a burden that is no longer peripheral but structural. High housing, energy, and everyday expenses are compressing real incomes, weighing on labor force participation, and dampening in-migration. The city remains expensive without the wage growth or employment dynamism typically required to sustain such pricing.

Against this backdrop, the broader economy appears orderly but weakening. The labor market has not broken, but momentum has clearly faded. Across major U.S. metros, San Diego now ranks near the bottom on several core indicators, pointing to relative underperformance rather than a cyclical pause.
