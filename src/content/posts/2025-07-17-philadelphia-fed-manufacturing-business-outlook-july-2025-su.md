Philadelphia Fed Manufacturing Business Outlook – July 2025 Summary

Manufacturing in the Mid-Atlantic region rebounded in July, with the general activity index turning positive for the first time since March. New orders and shipments hit their highest levels since February, and firms modestly increased hiring.

Expectations for future growth remain solid, with firms anticipating gains in orders, output, and capital spending. However, rising input costs (especially wages and health benefits) continue to weigh on sentiment, highlighting inflation as a key concern heading into year-end. (Cost-Push Inflation)

_______________________________________

Manufacturing Activity Rebounds

- General activity index rose to +15.9, turning positive after three straight negative readings — highest since February.

- New orders jumped to +18.4 and shipments surged to +23.7, both also at 5-month highs.

- Employment index climbed to +10.3, indicating overall job growth.

Labor and Compensation Trends

- 70% of firms reported no change in employment; 17% added jobs.

- 30% of firms raised wages in the past 3 months; none reduced them.

- Most firms (69%) are not changing 2025 compensation plans, but 28% plan to raise wages.

Price Pressures Rising Again

- Prices paid index surged from 41.4 to 58.8, suggesting stronger input cost pressures.

- Prices received rose to 34.8, with zero firms reporting price cuts on their goods.

Forward Outlook Still Positive

- Future general activity index improved slightly to 21.5.

- Firms remain optimistic about new orders (+30.0), employment (+20.1), and capital expenditures (+17.1).

- Wage growth expectations remain firm at 3–4%, and health benefit cost expectations rose slightly to 4–5%.
