Ramp's new AI Index is showing a very interesting shift in Applied AI. One, token usage is skyrocketing. Usage is growing exponentially. But cost has started to level off. This is part of the broader shift we are seeing in the AI marketplace.

First, what the Ramp report is showing:

- Volume is surging while revenue growth stalls: Token consumption jumped 37.1% between late September and late October, but total enterprise AI spending grew by only 8.9% in that same period

- Inference costs are collapsing: The blended price per million tokens dropped 26.8% month-over-month to just $0.56, driven largely by OpenAI slashing its API costs by 42.4%..

- The market relies heavily on a tiny fraction of power users: The median firm spends only $15.70 per employee per month on AI, while the top 1% of firms spend a massive $9,567 per employee, keeping overall revenues afloat despite plummeting per-token prices.

To qualify these numbers a bit, this index only measures U.S. businesses that use Ramp’s corporate card and bill-pay platforms to manage their expenses. The specific token volume and pricing metrics are drawn from a smaller subset of over 30,000 businesses actively using Ramp's billing tools.

This shift is interesting and important for a few reasons. One, the cost of inference is going down very fast. This is happening because the hardware side of the market has been flooded and has become hypercompetitive. You should be aware of your blended cost per 1m tokens, and it should be going down very quickly. Most surprising is how OpenAI and Anthropic dominated usage in this index, and the cost per token with them is also plummeting. That doesn't bode well for their IPOs, methinks.

Second, the SaaS flat rate pricing is going to fade away. Look at the difference between the average firm spending $16 per employee vs the top firms spending $9,500 per employee. AI is a utility, not software. You need to always price AI as a consumptive utility. Even though the price per token is going down, it will never be free. 100m tokens will always cost more than 10m tokens.

Third, no bubble is bursting. While falling token prices have stalled top-line revenue growth for model providers, enterprise integration is hardening. Ramp's economic data shows that enterprise retention rates for AI tools have climbed every year since 2022, and average enterprise AI contract sizes have exploded from $39,000 in 2023 to a projected $1 million in 2026. It is less of a bubble popping and more of a transition into massive, long-term operational commitments.

While this is just a snapshot of the market, the patterns are clear. AI usage is going way up, but the economics are transforming rapidly. The cost of solid AI is going way down, very quickly. The winners over the next 12 months will be companies that understand how to utilize tokens effectively and efficiently while building scalable and reliable Applied AI systems.
