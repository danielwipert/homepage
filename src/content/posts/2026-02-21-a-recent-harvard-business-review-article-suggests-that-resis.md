A recent Harvard Business Review article suggests that resistance to generative AI is being widely misread.

The obstacle is not technology, but psychology. As AI moves into cognitive and creative domains, it threatens workers’ sense of competence, autonomy, and belonging. The result is a quiet adoption gap: leaders embrace AI, while frontline use remains cautious, uneven, or concealed.

Where AI is introduced as an efficiency mandate, resistance is a rational response. Where it is framed as a redesign of work, adoption follows. This means emphasizing transparency, skill development, and human–AI complementarity.

The core insight is simple: generative AI is not a neutral tool inside organizations. It functions as a social actor. Treating it as such may determine whether AI initiatives deliver symbolic compliance or lasting transformation.
