I have finished Chapter 3 of my textbook, AI Operations Management. I am about three weeks beyond schedule because AI is expanding exponentially. I am finding myself having to edit and refine a chapter right after I write it.

That is kind of the point of a digital textbook. It is never set in stone. If new information comes to light, I will include and change my position and argument if needed. That is how knowledge actually evolves. While AI is moving fast, the core business principles that underpin its application are not. They are mostly bedrock.

I think the most surprising thing I have found in researching and writing is how quickly companies are evolving but how little planning is being done. There are lots of examples of companies reconfiguring and refining their Applied AI stack: AT&T, Rippling, Atlassian, etc. I honestly feel bad for companies that are not riding the crest of the wave. They are getting left behind. Some of these companies feel like they are leapfrogging others in their industry just by thinking critically and recursively about how they can actually use AI.

This was also the case during the rise of the internet. But I lived through that. It was not nearly as fast as how quickly AI is evolving. The change feels 10-20x as fast. The effects feel 50x as big. At the same time, the total lack of foresight is staggering. People are truly throwing spaghetti at the wall. The total lack of value attribution is mind-blowing. To be fair, we were at tokenmaxxing and leaderboards in Q1 this year. So maybe by 2027 companies might actually start to measure what the 10 million dollar invoice for tokens actually pays for?

The goal of writing this textbook is to give everyone a basic map to get there, to build the bridge between AI Use and Value Add. It is absolutely not easy, and the methods are not set in stone. But the goal and need is immutable. The absolute chaos of AI right now will blow over. But like a crazy night out, the bill and headache will come the morning after. I am trying to make the hangover a little less severe than it needs to be.
