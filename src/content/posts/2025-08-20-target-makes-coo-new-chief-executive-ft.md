Target makes COO new chief executive (FT)

Can Target pull out of the stall or will they sink towards Kmart/Sears...

I’ve wanted to write about Target for months. Their DEI reversal is destined to be a classic HBR case study. Any business undergrad could see this was a terrible move way before they did it.

For 25 years Target’s lane has been clear: the design-forward, progressive cousin to Walmart. Shoppers pick Target, because they want clean, well-run stores, on-trend basics, and fair prices, without the chaotic and uneasy Walmart vibe.

Then came the U-turn. In 2023 they yanked Pride merch from their shelves. In 2025, they ended their three year DEI goals and wound down REACH (the $2B Black-owned business push) while retreating from external DEI scorecards. That isn’t a campaign tweak, it rewrites the brand’s default behavior. The result: you don’t just anger activists, you confuse your core shopper. Every message now pays a credibility tax.

Why would a massive company like Target pick a fight with the LGBTQ and Black communities, core parts of its shopper base? I will have to write a full essay on this one, but some quick thoughts:

--Group Think-- I have no doubt the decisions to remove Pride merch and end DEI were mostly driven by a leadership team that has suppressed dissent in their ranks, which is the kiss of death. While capitalist corporate structure demands authoritarian hierarchy, you don't need to embellish it by silencing internal dissent. Good leaders don't silence people, they listen to them.

--Brand Identity Dysmorphia-- Brand Image is mutable, Brand Identity is much less so. The immediate hit to the brand image of Target is conspicuous: online criticism, lower sales, boycotts. The shift in Brand Identity is much more significant, longer-lasting, and potentially irreversible. Target is no longer trustworthy to many of its core consumers. Once you break trust, it is nearly impossible to get it back.

--Corporate Integrity-- When you stand for nothing, you fall for anything. Corporations are not immune to the real world of politics, religion, sexuality, etc. What Target did was basically insult and denigrate a huge swath of its customer base. They only way back is to win back their trust, which will taken herculean efforts from a progressive leadership team. Otherwise, Walmart and Amazon will gobble up their market share.
