Yesterday OpenAI dropped an atom bomb. It hasn't really set in, not even in the AI circles. In a short update to a help article they dumped on Halloween, while everyone was busy,  OpenAI confirmed one of the biggest changes in AI since its inception. AI is a utility, not software, and OpenAI is going to start charging all of us like the power company, by token.

When I read the subreddit message about this last night, I was perplexed. It only had like 200 upvotes, and the same story was not spreading to other AI subreddits. It was not all over Twitter. I had to reread the announcement a few times. Let's review exactly what OpenAI is doing:

- Plus, Pro, and Business plans will share a SINGLE usage allowance across ChatGPT and Codex starting in December 2026

- Allowances will be measured in TOKENS PROCESSED, rather than a flat number of messages

- Advanced reasoning models and complex tasks will drain the shared token budget much faster than simple queries

- Routine text conversations using only the Instant setting will remain unmetered

- Upon hitting the cap, users must wait for the reset window, purchase more tokens, or upgrade their subscription

I always dreamed of having my Big Short moment when I get to scream out, "There's a bubble... it's time to call bull*hit" This is probably the closest I am going to get.

In writing my textbook on AI Operations Management, I wrote on July 20th as the central thesis to the entire book: "Deployed AI use is resource consumption, not software access." OpenAI just confirmed my thesis.

If you are not fully following, here is the guy at the end of the bar version. The entire AI industry, all the billions and trillions, is being propped up by one bright shining lie. The lie is that AI is like every other piece of software or app you use. You pay for it once or you subscribe to it, and you can just use it however much you want. You pay for Excel once a year; you can use it 24/7 if you want. You pay for Netflix, and you can watch it every day all day. AI is not that. AI is something else entirely.

AI is consumptive. It requires a deep hardware stack in the background to run. And that usage is variable by the model and what you ask it. You ask, what is the weather tomorrow? Not a lot of tokens. If you give it a 40k row Excel sheet to analyze, lots of tokens. Until yesterday, OpenAI priced them the same. Not any more. Now the actual consumption cost is being charged, and it changes the entire industry.

Expect this to be the first domino to fall, and next, the AI hardware bubble will pop. Wall Street will realize that AI usage was being propped up by massive compute subsidies, from OpenAI and Anthropic. Once those subsidies disappear, you will have millions of GPUs gathering dust, and a lot of inference companies are going to go under.

Welcome to Dot Com Part Duex. AI is not going away, but it is going to be a totally different industry in a year.
