AI market is entering a "Show Me" phase. Broadcom dropped 13% today, here is my take on what it means for the broader AI market...

Let's get this out of the way. Is this the start of AI Crash? I don't think so, but that all depends on human behavior, which is always unpredictable.

Here is what I do know: Over the last few weeks, the cost of AI has become a hot topic. I will not recount all the headline stories; you have read most of them.

Sam Altman confirmed as much yesterday, speaking on the Cost of AI: "at the beginning of this year, an issue that never came up (people were totally happy with the amount they were spending) to, all of a sudden, a huge issue.”

The talk about AI is shifting from "I, for one, welcome our new Skynet overlords" to "Where's the money?" That all happened in the last 4 weeks. It feels like a paradigm shift.

Some quick points:

- AI is not going to die. This is the WWW all over again. It may not live up to the insane short-term utopianism. So what? AI will change the world.

- Tokenmaxxing and the entire concept of maxing out AI usage are done. That era is now DOA. It was bizarre and odd. Explain to future business majors how spending millions on a new, unproven tool with no way of tracking ROI is a good business tactic. You can't. It was delusional.

- AI Cost Management is the new song everyone will be singing. But that can't happen without ROI awareness and management. This is where we're going to hit some choppy air.

- Most companies are not ready for Gen AI. Their data is a mess. Their workflows are tangled. You can't measure ROI if those two pillars of management are not in a good place. This isn't garbage in, garbage out. This is a wastepaper basket overflowing, turning into a landfill. Gen AI will just exponentially magnify your current problems.

OK so what does this mean? AI is going to be a much slower sell than previously thought. It will take years to implement Gen AI at most businesses. That process will continue for decades.

For the market, this means:

- Broadcom’s drop is not evidence that the AI boom is over; it is evidence that the AI boom has been priced so aggressively that even a slight disappointment can erase hundreds of billions in market value.

- The safer side of the AI market is the cash-flow giants: Nvidia, Microsoft, Google, Amazon, Meta. They laid the tracks; they will run the trains.

- The highest risk companies now are: High valuation + high capex exposure + narrow customer base + unproven long-term margins + dependence on hyperscaler AI spending + stock already up massively  (I won't name them, but if you know, you know.)

AI demand is not collapsing, but AI valuations are losing their margin for error. The most vulnerable names are the ones priced for perfect, uninterrupted AI capex growth. A slowdown is catastrophic for them.
