Latest data from University of Michigan Customer Sentiment survey is out today. The political subset of that data has some interesting shifts...

I have been reviewing the political breakdown of this data for the last few months. The topline story has been that Dems are apoplectic about the economy since Trump took office. Republicans are ecstatic. That has not changed. The severity goes up and down slightly, but Dems and GOP are living in totally different realities from each other. The gap between Dems and GOP was the highest ever in the survey in   April this year with the 2nd highest gap coming last month in August.

Independents are obviously somewhere in the middle. But the latest data is signally a clear shift towards a more negative viewpoint of the economy. We can see this in all 3 indexes U of Michigan measures, which looks at current and future expectations. Even during dark days of Covid, the Indy sentiment hovered around 70, but now their expectation of future economics has tanked to 47. For most of 2025, Independents have been very negative about the economy.

While not as filled with rage and despair as Democrats, Independents are not living in La La Land like GOP voters. This negative shift for Independents suggests the overall economic mood in the United States has calcified into anger. People used the 2024 election as a referendum on the economy, and they have not gotten what they voted for, quite the opposite. Things are so bad that the GOP voter is starting to shift down. Their ecstatic 107 expectation rating in Dec 2024 has dipped below 100 to 98 in September. Perhaps they are beginning to understand how tariffs actually work.
