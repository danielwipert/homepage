As I have been researching and writing my textbook, AI Operations Management, I get the fear that maybe what I am saying won't matter in 12 months. AI is moving fast, and the economics are like a Pollock painting. My biggest fear is that my main thesis is just wrong. Maybe AI is not a utility; maybe the cost will drop so low that it will replicate the SaaS pricing model, where you buy per seat, token consumption is an afterthought.

I have become a huge believer in facing fears the moment they pop up. I have lived enough decades doing the opposite to know how that plays out. So will the cost of AI drop so much that the whole concept of pricing tokens won't even matter for most business users? The facts look promising for that evolution. The cost per token is dropping for the same level of intelligence. Look at GPT-4 which premiered in March 2023 at $30 per 1M tokens, which eventually dropped to $.15 per 1M tokens in 2026. The same intelligence dropped 99.5% in three years.

Does this mean that pricing AI like a utility is just a short-term viewpoint, and in the long run, AI costs will drop so low that it could be priced as SaaS?

Counterpoint 1 - The Jevons Paradox

The Jevons Paradox states that increases in efficiency lead to increases in consumption. In AI operations, cheap tokens do not reduce IT budgets. Instead, they unlock compute-intensive architectures that were previously cost prohibitive. As token costs go down, consumption goes way up in volume and by task. This also points out the biggest flaw of the zero-cost argument. The token is not a unit of value. The unit of value can be defined as the cost to complete a task, and that cost is highly variable to the task at hand.

Counterpoint 2 - Inference Will Never Be Free

Calling AI compute "free" because you leased the server is a financial sleight of hand. Shifting a cost from variable to fixed doesn't stop the clock on silicon that turns into scrap every four years, nor does it trick the power company. Today’s dirt-cheap intelligence isn't a technological miracle. It’s just Big Tech burning billions to buy market share, a massive subsidy masquerading as a revolution right up until the bill comes due.

Counterpoint 3 - AI is not software

Traditional SaaS was a miracle: write code once, sell it a million times, keep the cash. AI turns that arithmetic inside out. A flat $30 monthly subscription means a casual user costs pennies, but a power user running continuous agent loops burns $500 in GPU time by Tuesday. Flat-rate AI isn't high-margin software. It’s an adverse-selection trap where your best customers bankrupt you, forcing founders to roll out throttling tiers and admit their sleek SaaS disguise is just a metered utility bill.

I am still bullish on my thesis. AI is about intelligence, not compute. The lingua franca will be the finalized, polished output. Even as models get cheaper and more optimized, the expected output will only increase in difficulty and cost.
