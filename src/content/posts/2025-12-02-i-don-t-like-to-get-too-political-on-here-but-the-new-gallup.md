I don't like to get too political on here, but the new Gallup poll from Friday mirrors what we are seeing with U of Michigan Customer Sentiment Survey...

I wrote last week about how the long standing impenetrable fortress of GOP optimism was starting to weaken in the monthly University of Michigan Survey of Customer Sentiment. It wasn't a big drop in economic confidence, but any drop amongst GOP voters is a huge change. GOP voters were basically giving Trump and his admin a free pass.

Gallup released their poll on Trump's approval rating last week. His overall approval tanked to 36%, which his lowest approval rating in his 2nd term. The only other time he was that low was right after the Jan 6 Insurrection at the end of his first term.

More telling and interesting was the shift in GOP approval for Trump. GOP voters have been steadfast in their loyal support and approval of Trump. It seemed as if nothing could shake their faith in Trump. Yet last month, the dam began to break.

Trump's approval rating amongst GOP voters has held at a 91% average throughout all of 2025. The last few months it had even jumped a bit to 93% approval. In Nov, the GOP approval for Trump dropped down to 84%. That doesn't sound terrible, but it indicates that Teflon Don is started to have problems with his most loyal supporters.

Why this is happening is a question for political science experts. But much like the data from last month's U of Michigan Customer Sentiment survey, the GOP voters are starting to show doubt and concern about the direction of the country and how Trump is leading the country. This shift could have a huge effect on the macroeconomic climate in the United States.
