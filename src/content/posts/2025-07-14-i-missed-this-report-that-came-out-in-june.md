I missed this report that came out in June. Some more not so great news, but  it indicates that we are in an odd and unique economic situation that has been occurring for years now.

The Conference Board Leading Economic Index (LEI) is highly regarded as a predictive signal for economic health:

- Leading indicators (LEI) - targeting 10 factors such as weekly manufacturing hours, unemployment claims, housing permits, the S&P 500, credit spreads, consumer expectations are designed to predict economic turns about 7 months ahead.

- Over the past six months, the LEI has declined 2.7%, accelerating its downward trend from the earlier six-month.

- The steep six-month decline and weak diffusion among its components have triggered the Conference Board’s “recession signal”

Ok so not great news, but looking back at historicals, something odd is happening. The LEI recession signal pinged during Dot.com Crash in early 2000s, Great Recession 2008, and Covid. That's it in the last 25 years. Except the recession signal has been pinging constantly since 2022. Something is quite off here.

I am not economist (although I did ace my econ class at Queen Mary University) so I don't know exactly why the LEI has been signaling a future recessions basically for the last three years straight, which is longer than it did for any of the previous actual recessions we had. What has been happening since 2022 that is causing this?

In reviewing reports over the last few months, I have noticed a weird inflection point around the first six months of 2022 where the economy appears to have transformed. Job openings peaked in early 2022 and never really recovered. A lot of this was Covid reshuffling of industries and roles, but we had 12 million open jobs in Jan 2022 to now less than 8 million. That is a massive swing.

Another shifted in 2022 was from economic polling. Civiqs is a great free resource for this. They ask people "Do you think the nation's economy is getting better or worse?" This tanked to 57% saying the economy was worse as covid hit in early 2020, but it then rebounded immediately. But later in 2022 it tanked again down to 62% saying the economy was getting worse in June of that year. Why? Inflation reached a 40-year high in June 2022, with the Consumer Price Index (CPI) rising 9.1% year-over-year.

I don't have the answer, but I do find this all very curious. The LEI has been signally a future recession since early 2022. This is longest stretch of recession signals since before 2000. We are in uncharted waters now. This is different than Dot.com Crash, Great Recession, and Post-Covid. We are in an extended downturn that isn't officially a recession, but feels very much like a recession. The rules have changed, and we don't know what they are. We also have no idea where this is going.
