U of Michigan Survey of Customers - Preliminary Results for July 2025  
The July 2025 value of 61.8 is well below the long-term average (which historically hovers around 85–90).

Consumer sentiment remains historically depressed, and July’s modest rebound does little to signal broad confidence recovery 📉

Comparable lows were observed:  
  • Early 1980s recession  
  • 2008–2009 global financial crisis  
  • 2022 sentiment trough during post-pandemic inflation shocks

"Consumers are unlikely to regain their confidence in the economy unless they feel assured that inflation is unlikely to worsen, for example if trade policy stabilizes for the foreseeable future."

- Consumer sentiment rose slightly to 61.8, the highest in five months but still 16% below December 2024 and well under historical norms.

- Views on short-term business conditions improved (+8%), but expectations for personal finances declined (-4%).

- Year-ahead inflation expectations fell for a second straight month, plunging from 5.0% last month to 4.4% this month.

- Long-run inflation expectations receded for the third consecutive month, falling back from 4.0% in June to 3.6% in July.

Both readings are the lowest since February 2025 but remain above December 2024, indicating that consumers still perceive substantial risk that inflation will increase in the future.
