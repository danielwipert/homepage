A direct line exists from a World War 2 anti-aircraft gun to you using ChatGPT as your weekly shrink. Spend the next 6 minutes with me to trace that 90-year journey from Cybernetics to ChatGPT.

### The Origins of Cybernetics

The Allies had a problem in World War 2. Their anti-aircraft guns were missing German plans. The guns were not able to predict where a plane would fly next to fire a leading shot so the bullet would hit the plane. Enter polymath Norbert Wiener. He was tasked with solving the problem and came up with a rather elegant yet simple solution. The targeting system for the gun needs to see its own errors, recalibrate, then try again. This is the birth of the Feedback Loop, Cybernetics, and the entire thesis behind my newsletter.

The funny thing is, Norbert Wiener was wrong. His idea of creating a self-correcting anti-aircraft gun failed. It was too hard to predict the actions of a human pilot. But the concept fired up Wiener, and he went on to write C*ybernetics: Or Control and Communication in the Animal and the Machine* in 1948. If I am losing you, just hang in there, because it is imperative that you understand this. **Cybernetics **is *the study of control and communication in machines and living things*. Its core concept is the **Feedback Loop**, *a system that measures its own output and uses that measurement to adjust its next move*. This is where you need to start if you really want to understand how AI actually works.

### The Dartmouth Foil

While Wiener was developing his eccentric concept of Cybernetics, a group of stuffy East Coast intellectuals felt compelled to counter the philosophical bent of Wiener with a more grounded, logic-based system. In the summer of 1956, at a Dartmouth College workshop, a group of computer pioneers and mathematicians created the idea of **Artificial Intelligence**. The Dartmouth group had set out to codify all human intelligence into a set of complex rules that could be inputted into a computer, and in turn, the computer could understand, reason, and act like a human. While Cybernetics was more focused on creating stable systems of symbiosis, the creators of Artificial Intelligence were obsessed with creating a thinking machine out of a set of complex rules.

The conference at Dartmouth birthed what we later called **Symbolic AI**, *a rule-based approach where you map reality into code, feed it to a computer, and let it execute toward a specific target.* This was the main focus of Artificial Intelligence for most of the 20th century. But there was one big problem. Reality is hard to encode. Like Cybernetics, the initial excitement of AI never translated to real-world results. Just like today, massive checks were written. Promises were made. Most of it ended in abject failure. This all led to what we call the **AI Winters**, where funding collapsed in the late 1970s and then again in the late 1980s.

### Letting Go of Control

In the 1990s, Symbolic AI began to wane as a new concept emerged. Instead of trying to encode the entire world, we began to let the machines find the rules themselves. This was **Machine Learning**, *a system that learns patterns from examples instead of hand-coded rules*. The shift here is immense. The Dartmouth concept was wrong, and the failures in artificial intelligence pre-2010 demonstrate this. You cannot write a rule for every possible input. But you could have computers read the output and then write rules on their own. Sounds familiar, like Norbert Wiener’s anti-aircraft gun. A system that reads its own outputs and then corrects itself towards a desired goal. That is Cybernetics 101.

Machine learning opened up a whole new world for artificial intelligence. People started dusting off old ideas and repurposing them. **Neural Networks **had been around since the 1950s, but they had always been on the fringe. To put it simply, *a neural network is a brain-inspired computing system of layered, interconnected units built to learn from data*. Just think of them as nodes that data passes through. If you have a lot of layers of these nodes, then we call it **Deep Learning**. That’s it, not really that hard to understand. The magic happens when you use deep learning within neural networks to train on massive data sets and let them make their own rules.

### Right Here, Right Now

Cybernetics was about creating machines that could function in the real world. Classical Artificial Intelligence was about creating machines that could mimic the real world. Both were right and wrong. The world is much too complex to fully mimic via rules and code. So we let the machines read the world, find the patterns, and create their own rules and codes. That is where we are now. Cybernetics mixed with classical Artificial Intelligence gets us to ChatGPT.

We have been dreaming about a thinking machine for almost a century now. We have made massive leaps forward in the last decade. But the desire is the same as it was in the 1940s. We want a machine to help make life better, make life easier. That is what Norbert Wiener was building, and that is what the eggheads at Dartmouth envisioned in the 1950s: computers helping humans. The difference between then and now is that the dream is no longer a dream. It is right here, right now.

But, of course, with great power comes great responsibility. To fully embrace that responsibility, we need to understand what AI is and how it actually works. AI is a computer, a computer that can review data and create rules based on that data. Then, if we allow it to, AI can act on the rules it has created. AI is not some bogeyman or civilization destroyer. It can only do what we let it and want it to do.

The key takeaway from this little intro into the history of cybernetics is this: artificial intelligence has always been a push and pull between human trust and the power of computers. AI failed in the 20th century because we didn’t trust and/or believe in the power of computing. We operated under the assumption that a computer needed to be spoon-fed every single detail before it could process a task. It turns out computers can do a lot on their own. In the 21st century, we have let computers run wild with neural networks, deep learning, and large language models. The last decade of experimentation has culminated in a moment of clarity. Computers can be much more powerful than the human mind, and that is terrifying. But it shouldn’t be. A car can travel 50x farther than a human being in a single day. Should I be scared of my Subaru?

Now is the moment to harness the immense power of Generative AI, not be afraid of it. It is like a jet engine, and all we have is a horse and buggy. Gen AI will not work with our current systems. Using Gen AI without new systems would be like strapping a jet engine onto a carriage. We need to build a plane around the new jet engine we have created. That will take time, patience, and a dedication to safe, reliable, and trustworthy artificial intelligence.
