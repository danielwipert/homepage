City Economic Health Report | TAMPA | B-

The Tampa–St. Petersburg–Clearwater metropolitan area ranks below the national median, with a weighted percentile score of 44 in the latest economic assessment. While the unemployment rate of 5.0% appears moderate, it masks a deeper weakness in labor supply. Labor force participation stands at just 60.0%, placing the region in the 6th percentile among major US metropolitan areas, a level more typical of retirement-heavy or low-engagement labor markets than of high-growth urban economies.

This reflects long-standing demographic and structural features of the region. Tampa’s population skews older than the national average, with a large share of retirees and near-retirees mechanically depressing participation rates. At the same time, the local economy remains heavily weighted toward sectors such as tourism, hospitality and healthcare support, which tend to offer lower attachment to full-time work and higher levels of informal or seasonal employment. The result is an economy that can post respectable headline employment figures while drawing from a comparatively shallow pool of active workers.

Wage growth remains a notable counterpoint. Earnings rose 8.3% over the past year, placing the region in the 82nd percentile nationally. This suggests that firms are competing more aggressively for a constrained labor supply, rather than drawing strength from expanding workforce participation.

Housing indicators reinforce the picture of adjustment. Home prices fell 4.16% year on year, signalling a cooling market, while building permits surged 71.38%, pointing to continued investment tied to population inflows rather than immediate sales demand. With homes spending a median of 80 days on the market, pricing power has clearly shifted toward buyers.

Overall, the data point to an economy supported by population inflows and wage increases, but constrained by low labor engagement, a combination that limits upside growth and raises questions about longer-term productive capacity.
