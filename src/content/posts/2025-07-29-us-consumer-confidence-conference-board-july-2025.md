US Consumer Confidence (Conference Board, July 2025)

"Consumers are turning more cautious amid election uncertainty and slower job growth. If expectations remain below 80, the risk of a recession in early 2026 will increase.”

Headlines

- Expectations Index (based on consumers’ short-term outlook for income, business, and labor market condition) rose 4.5 points to 74.4. But expectations remained below the threshold of 80 that typically signals a recession ahead for the sixth consecutive month.

- Appraisal of current job availability weakened for the seventh consecutive month, reaching its lowest level since March 2021

- Consumer Confidence Index improved by 2.0 points in July to 97.2 (1985=100), from 95.2 in June (revised up by 2.2 points) July’s modest gain in confidence was driven by consumers over 35 years old and shared across all income groups except those earning the least (with household annual income below $15K).

"Consumers’ write-in responses showed that tariffs remained top of mind and were mostly associated with concerns that they would lead to higher prices."
