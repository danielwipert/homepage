Where is Agentic AI going? The current assumption is more agents equals more intelligence equals better outcomes. A new paper suggests otherwise.

Recent work shows that adding agents often introduces coordination costs that outweigh any marginal gains in capability. In sequential reasoning tasks, performance can degrade materially as additional models fragment the logic chain. What looks like collaboration is often just noise.

Where multi-model systems do work, they follow a stricter discipline. Centralized orchestration outperforms loosely coupled agents. Verification layers matter more than generation layers. And most importantly, the system decides when not to orchestrate at all.

This is the uncomfortable truth: complexity is not intelligence. It is overhead.  
The real frontier is not building bigger agent collectives, but designing systems that know when to stay simple, when to decompose, and how to enforce verification when they do.

In other words, the future of AI systems looks less like a crowd, and more like a well-oiled machine.
