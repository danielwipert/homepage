Pittsburgh, PA scored an A+ on my City Economic Score for April 2026. Here is why...

--Unemployment & Labor Market--

Pittsburgh’s unemployment rate of 3.6% sits within the healthy 3.5–4.5% range, indicating a tight labour market. However, a 0.3 percentage point year-over-year increase points to modest softening, suggesting some erosion in labour market resilience.

--Workforce Supply--

The labour force is expanding 0.73% year-over-year, a steady pace that supports economic activity without creating immediate supply constraints. This helps offset demographic headwinds typical of older industrial metros.

--Wage Growth--

Average hourly earnings are rising 8.01% year-over-year, well above inflation. This implies meaningful real income gains, likely driven by tight conditions or wage catch-up dynamics, though sustained growth may introduce inflationary pressure.

--Labor Demand--

Labor demand remains firm, with a composite score of 6.96 and employment growth of 1.07%. Weekly hours are 1.17% above the 12-month baseline, indicating employers are extending hours, often a precursor to further hiring.

--Cost of Living--

A cost of living score of 1.0 underscores strong affordability, particularly in housing. Despite moderate wages, purchasing power remains elevated, enhancing Pittsburgh’s appeal for both residents and inbound migration.

--Office Economy--

An Office Worker Ratio of 3.46 reflects a well-established white-collar base, signaling a durable shift toward knowledge sectors such as healthcare and education, with positive implications for productivity and wages.

--Housing — Construction--

Building permits have fallen 10.55% year-over-year, a sharp contraction that points to weakening developer activity. This limits future supply and risks tightening the market despite current affordability.

--Housing — Market Velocity--

Median days on market have risen to 65, up 4.84% year-over-year. This indicates slowing transaction velocity, consistent with softening demand rather than overheating conditions.

--Conclusion--

Pittsburgh’s economy remains stable, underpinned by strong wage growth, solid labour demand, and high affordability. However, early signs of labour market softening and a sharp pullback in construction suggest emerging risks. Housing momentum is fading, and without new supply, sustained wage gains could begin to pressure availability and pricing over time.
