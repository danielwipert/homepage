So inflation... The data has been very clear for the last few months that companies were going to raise prices. I have highlighted on here many times that the regional Fed business surveys were all telling the same story. Costs were going up for businesses, but they were not yet fully passing through to customer prices. This is the Price Paid vs Price Received gap. That gap has been at historical highs recently in several different regional business surveys.

I point this out for a couple of reason. One, I was right (I am a leo afterall). Two, it wasn't that hard to read or predict. The signals have been crystal clear for months. Inflation is going up for a few reasons:

- Tariffs - A tariff is a tax paid by business importing goods into the USA. The Trump trade policy has suddenly and significantly raised the cost to sell goods to people. While a lot of companies, especially big ones, could hold off on customer price increases for a quarter or two, increased costs have now hurt margins too much to stomach. Companies are raising prices across the board in pretty much every sector due to tariffs.

- Shelter - This accounts for a 1/3 of the CPI and it was a big mover in August. It is also a lagging indicator, because it looks at rental leases signed 6 months ago. It is going up because there is a very tight housing supply in USA. We are millions of units short to meet supply. So while we are seeing headlines like housing market and rental markets are cooling, the numbers just do not indicate that yet on a national level. They might be cooling relative to where they were before but the prices are still going up on a whole.

- Food - We are all seeing this one. It is broken down between Food at Home vs Getting Food Out. Food at Home has seen some cooling of inflation, but we don't feel that because grocery food prices have exploded since covid (up about 30% since 2020 vs 22% overall inflation). Food Away from Home has been bonkers, up 37% since 2020. The inputs to these increases are very complex, but the average person doesn't care. All we see is that the thing we need to survive has become much more expensive, and it is seems to be getting worse. Wages inflation has not kept up with food inflation.

So what now? Rate cut in September is still probably happening but 50 basis points is off the table, we should expect 25 basis points. Rest of the year, I think you know the answer. Data never gives us a perfect prediction, but the signals are very loud and clear where inflation is going.
