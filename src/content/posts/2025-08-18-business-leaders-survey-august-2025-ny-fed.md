Business Leaders Survey - August 2025 (NY Fed)

“Business activity declined for a sixth straight month, Employment was little changed, and price increases were widespread. Firms turned pessimistic about the outlook.” — Richard Deitz, New York Fed.

Activity: “Business activity continued to decline” with the headline index –11.7; the climate “remained negative at –39.3.”

Labor: “Employment was little changed,” while “wage growth was modest.”

Prices: “The prices paid index remained elevated… the prices received index had its highest reading since March of 2023,” indicating a “small pickup in selling price increases.”

Outlook: “Firms turned pessimistic about the outlook.” Future prices received hit “its highest level since 2022,” implying widespread price increases ahead; "Capital spending plans were soft."

-- Weak demand, stable headcount. Activity is down again, but firms aren’t cutting aggressively (“employment was little changed”), which signals defensive holding pattern, not layoffs

-- Margins: slight relief. Prices received at a post-2023 high suggests better pass-through versus earlier this year, something to watch if it persists. Margin Crush is relieved, but this means... INFLATION!

--So what?--

- Forecast conservatively: Plan for softer revenue, steady staffing.
- Price strategy: If you lagged on pass-through, this may be a window to calibrate modest increases.
- Supply risk: “Supply availability… worsened.” Lock in critical inputs early.
