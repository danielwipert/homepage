Dallas Fed Texas Service Sector Outlook Survey -- July 2025

Texas service firms report renewed growth in July, but a stubborn gap between gloomy present sentiment and hopeful future expectations signals a stalled, wait-and-see economy.

Key Highlights

- Revenue index jumped 10 points to +6.3, signaling renewed expansion.
- Employment index turned positive at +2.8 (up from –1.2), and hours worked rose to +3.2
- General business activity swung back to +2.0, though the company outlook stayed slightly negative at –2.1
- Outlook uncertainty eased to 12.8, still above average.

Cost & Wage Pressures  
• Input prices climbed further to +25.3, indicating rising costs (seeing this across the board in regional Fed reports)  
• Selling price index cooled to +3.3, reflecting slower pass-through to customers. Aligns with other Fed reports, Margin Crush is happening now, inflation coming later this year, no doubt.  
• Wages & benefits growth picked up to +13.4, up from 8.6% last month

Future Expectations  
• Future general business activity outlook improved to +9.8 (from +1.5) -- Highest since Feb 25  
• Future revenue index remained strong at +31.3, down a bit from June (12-month trailing avg is 37).

The Outlook Discrepancy

When reviewing the data tables, I noticed a perplexing data schism between Company Outlook vs Future Company Outlook. What does that actually mean? The first is a MoM measurement, the latter is 6 months looking forward. The data is showing us that companies are negative about their current situation and have been since Feb 25 (tariffs!). The index here is avg -8.2 since Feb.

But they are consistently optimistic about their companies looking 6 months ahead. AVG +8 over the last three months. Something doesn't add up here. Companies are resolutely negative about their current situation and have been for months, but they are still optimistic about the future?

This is a sign of stalled economy. It is not good in reality right now, but they are hopeful that things will improve. Hope is not reality though. This means companies are not making big moves now with it comes to CapEx or OpEx.

Wait and See, aka Cross-Your-Fingers economy.
