A team at EPFL has built what may be the first fully open clinical AI model, where every step of the training process is documented, inspectable, and open to scrutiny.

Led by Xavier Theimer-Lienhard, the team adapted five open foundation models into medical specialists using a pipeline they call Fully Open Meditron. The process combines eight public medical datasets with clinician-vetted synthetic data, including question-answer pairs grounded in 46,469 clinical practice guidelines.

Their best model, Apertus-70B-MeditronFO, improved by 6.6 percentage points over its base model on aggregate medical benchmarks, setting a new state of the art among fully open systems. But the finding is narrower than the headline suggests.

The pipeline relies heavily on synthetic data generated by a single teacher model, GPT-OSS-120B, which accounts for roughly 64% of the training examples. Clinician panels vetted the generation prompts, but reviewed only three sampled outputs per template. That still leaves room for item-level errors, bias, and hidden failure modes.

So the real takeaway is not “open clinical AI has arrived." It is this: Fully open clinical AI pipelines can now be competitive enough to deserve serious attention. But they are not automatically safe, complete, or production-ready.

For leaders evaluating AI in clinical support, the implication is less about raw capability and more about vendor risk.

A model whose training data and process are fully disclosed is inherently more auditable than a black-box specialist. That matters for regulatory compliance, internal governance, and understanding what the model has actually learned.

The trade-off is clear: open models may still lag the best proprietary systems, but they offer something proprietary systems usually do not: inspectability.  
That is the procurement question.

Do you want a more powerful system you cannot fully examine, or a somewhat less capable system whose training process you can actually audit?

Worth reading if you are responsible for clinical AI procurement, governance, or risk assessment.

Worth ignoring if your only metric is benchmark performance.

Paper: Fully Open Meditron: An Auditable Pipeline for Clinical LLMs, Theimer-Lienhard et al.
