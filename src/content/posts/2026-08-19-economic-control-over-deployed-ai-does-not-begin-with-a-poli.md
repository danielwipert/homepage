Economic control over deployed AI does not begin with a policy or a decision. It begins with something management can actually act on. A spending limit needs usage it can limit. A cost-allocation rule needs activity it can assign. A monitor needs something to watch. An evaluation needs costs and outcomes it can compare.

That gives economic control a surprisingly simple prerequisite: the organization must define the boundary of the activity and measure what happens inside it. What costs count? What value counts? Over what period? Against which outcomes? Once those lines are drawn, management has something to govern.

Without the boundary, the numbers have no meaning. Without measured activity, there is nothing to control. The company may have dashboards, invoices, and plenty of awareness about AI usage. But awareness is not control. Control begins when management can connect a specific piece of activity to a cost, a value, and a decision it can make.
