One of the biggest friction points right now is how human beings and AI overlap. This is not just about work automation or AI slop. It is the demarcation between what a machine does well vs. what a human does well. Right now, that line feels like no man's land. It should not be. We can't move forward without facing it.

A new paper released yesterday attempts to walk the tightrope between AI and human work: "AI Article Reviews Dominate Human Peer Reviews at Their Best." Researchers generated AI reviews of 124 research manuscripts and compared them with 134 reviews written by paid human experts. AI judges evaluated the reviews overall and across seven dimensions, while three former journal editors independently compared a random sample of ten review pairs without knowing which reviews were AI-generated.

Human editors preferred the AI review in 87% of their blinded judgments. AI reviews also scored higher across all seven review dimensions, with 5.5% of audited claims judged to contradict the manuscripts, compared with 15.9% for human reviews. In short, AI produced research feedback that human reviewers found more helpful than human feedback. AI also made fewer factual misreadings of the manuscripts than humans.

These reviews are highly skilled work. They involve assessing arguments, spotting weaknesses, and explaining what needs improvement. The fact that experienced editors preferred AI’s feedback without knowing its source suggests AI can perform a meaningful part of that work. But before we hand the keys over to HAL and Skynet, we need to be realistic.

AI is not popular, at all. The SV bubble thinks it's God, while people east of Livermore think it is quite possible the devil. I know people that don't even want to see or hear anything related to AI, and they summarily reject all AI outputs. These are not crackpots either. A paper and study like this will only exacerbate the social and personal objections to AI, especially as it enters the workforce. At the same time, the facts are the facts. This paper proves AI can do very difficult and complex work better than people.

I am constantly trying to thread the needle on here, at work, and in my personal life between AI doomerism and utopianism. The reality is that AI is incredibly powerful, and this paper is further proof of it. AI can do insane things, especially in terms of research and textual analysis. But AI is not an answer. It is a means to an end.

Threading the needle means accepting the power of AI without worshipping it. And it means not turning your back on a technology that can make our lives so much better. This paper is another blaze on the path forward towards useful and reliable AI.
