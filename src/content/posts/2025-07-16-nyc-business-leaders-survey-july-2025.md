NYC Business Leaders Survey – July 2025

In July 2025, business activity in the New York–Northern New Jersey service sector continued to decline but at a slower pace, with the activity index rising slightly to -9.3. The business climate remained poor, though less negative than in June. Employment and wages both showed modest gains, and selling prices rose at the fastest pace in over two years. Input costs stayed high, while supply chain conditions improved slightly but remain strained. Overall, firms shifted from a pessimistic to a neutral outlook, signaling tentative stabilization but no strong recovery yet.

--Good News--

- Pace of Decline Slowed: Business activity declined for the fifth month, but the rate of decline eased. The activity index rose 4 points to -9.3.

- Employment Ticked Up: The employment index increased to 3.8, showing modest job gains.

- Wage Growth Accelerated: The wages index jumped 9 points to 28.3—suggesting a noticeable rebound in pay growth.

- Outlook Stabilized: Firms are no longer pessimistic about the future; the forward-looking business activity index turned slightly positive at 3.7.

--Bad News--

- Business Climate Remains Weak: The business climate index, though improved, remained deeply negative at -34.6. Nearly half of firms still say conditions are worse than normal.

- Activity Still Contracting: Despite the slower pace of decline, the region is still seeing negative activity readings for the fifth straight month.

- Cost Pressures Persist: The prices paid index remains high at 64.5, indicating continued input cost inflation.

--Mixed News--

- Capital Spending Plans Soft: The index for capital spending rose from -12.6 to -8.5, an improvement but still a negative signal.

- Future Expectations Tepid: Firms are no longer pessimistic, but they’re not exactly optimistic either. Forward-looking indicators show weak confidence in a robust recovery.

- Input vs. Output Pricing Gap: Selling prices are rising, but still lag behind the elevated pace of input costs, squeezing margins for some firms.
