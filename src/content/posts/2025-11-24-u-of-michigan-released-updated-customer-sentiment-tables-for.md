U of Michigan released updated Customer Sentiment tables for Political Affiliation. The GOP Fort Knox of Optimism is weakening...

I have been following and writing about the University of Michigan survey of Customer Sentiment for a most of the year. Customer Sentiment is a very important indicator on the health of the economy. We have been seeing very poor numbers here since the Tariff Wars began earlier this year.

What’s especially interesting is how GOP voters have been shielded from this wave of negativity. As I have been saying, it feels like GOP Voters are living in La La Land. But this is me being glib to some degree. GOP Voters have more money. This economy is booming at the top, so it makes sense that GOP would be skewed positive over Independents and Democrats. But their resolute optimism seemed very misplaced.

In the latest U of Michigan release, we are starting to see the optimism bubble pop with GOP voters

- The overall GOP Customer Sentiment Index has fallen from 97 to 87 in a single month. It is currently at its lowest since Dec 24, before Trump took office.

- GOP viewpoint of current economic conditions has tanked going from 92 in Oct, down to 79 in Nov. This resets the GOP positivity that took over when Trump took office. This is a massive drop.

- The most resilient metric for GOP lately has been their view of the economic future. That too has begun to collapse in the current economic maelstrom. This has hovered right at 100 for most of the year. In Nov, it dove to 91. The lowest reading since the 2024 election.

I knew this would eventually happen. Reality has a funny way of sneaking up to people. We can push it away for a long time, but it will always break through: Tariffs, layoffs, AI, affordability crisis. Those all affect GOP voters like any other person in America.
