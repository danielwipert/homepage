CONSUMER PRICE INDEX – JUNE 2025

- The Consumer Price Index rose 0.3% in June, up from 0.1% in May, bringing the 12-month inflation rate to 2.7%.

- Core inflation (excluding food and energy) also climbed 0.2%, keeping the core annual rate steady at 2.9%.

- Driving the increase? Shelter, food, and energy—with gasoline prices reversing course (+1.0%) and food at home seeing consistent monthly gains. A little consumer relief showed up: used cars, new vehicles, and airline fares all declined.

What Stands Out:

- Over the past 12 months, shelter costs have increased by 3.8%, which is a substantial rise given that the overall CPI is up 2.7%.

- In June 2025, electricity prices rose 3.3% and piped natural gas prices rose 1.5% month-over-month. These are unusually large single-month increases for utilities, which are essential and typically more stable. This kind of spike can significantly impact household budgets and business operating costs.

- Food inflation is stable but uneven: Volatile items like eggs are cooling dramatically, but staples like beverages, produce, and sugar are trending up. For consumers, that means grocery bills may not feel cheaper, even if inflation looks “under control” at a macro level.
