Philadelphia Fed’s July 2025 Nonmanufacturing Business Outlook Survey signals a Turnaround...

July’s data points to a clear stabilization in the Third District’s service economy. Businesses are gaining traction on activity and employment fronts, even as inflation remains a factor. This signals a plateau in contraction: businesses are adapting, hiring cautiously, and passing through costs. The six‐month outlook remains favorable.

--Headlines--

- Recovery in activity and demand suggests a rebound after a prolonged contraction phase.
- Labor demand is firming, with full-time hires and extended workweeks reflecting business confidence.
- Inflationary pressures remain with cost increases across inputs, outputs, and labor, a sign of persistent upward price momentum.
- Forward-looking sentiment is cautiously optimistic, suggesting stability and gradual improvement.

Activity & Orders Rebound

- General activity jumped from –17.3 to +10.2 , marking the first positive reading in six months and the strongest level since last October
- New‑orders index surged by 30 points to +9.7, also the highest since October

Sales & Staffing Show Growth

- Sales/revenues climbed to +11.8, their highest reading in eight months
- Full‑time employment moved positive at +4.1, while the average workweek increased to +12.5, signaling rising labor demand

Input & Selling Prices on the Rise

- Input costs (prices paid) increased to +32.5, and prices received edged up to +12.4 (the highest since last December) reflecting sustained inflationary pressure
- If these cost pressures persist or accelerate, especially in a tight labor market, consumer prices will likely follow with a lag, and the gradual increase could turn into a faster climb.

Wage Pressures Persist

- Over 35% of firms have raised wages in this month vs only 4% decreasing wages, nearly 37% plan further increases
- 50% of firms surveyed expect pay to rise MORE than 3% in 2025

Forward Outlook Remains Positive

- The future general activity index slightly decreases to +11.7, with 39% of firms anticipating growth in the next six months
