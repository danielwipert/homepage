I have been reading John Cassidy's book about the dot-com crash in the early 2000s. His intro anecdote about Priceline is kind of mind-blowing...

Priceline did their IPO in March 1999, a year before the crash began:

"They were issued at $16 each, but the price immediately jumped to $85. At the close of trading, the stock stood at $68; it had risen 425 percent on the day. Priceline.com was valued at almost $10 billion—more than United Airlines, Continental Airlines and Northwest Airlines combined.

Airlines like United and Continental own valuable terminals, landing slots, and well-known brand names—not to mention their planes. Priceline.com owned some software, a couple of powerful computers, and an untested brand name."

This was a company that was operating at a staggering yearly loss of over 100 million per year. Yet people with college degrees and experts in finance bought up their shares like it was the last lifeboat off the Titanic. Then reality crashed the party:

"A few weeks after Priceline.com’s IPO, its stock reached $150, at which point the tiny company was worth more than the entire U.S. airline industry. Two years later, the stock was trading at less than $2, and the entire market capitalization of Priceline.com would not have covered the cost of two Boeing 747s."

We all know AI investment is a bubble right now. AI itself is not, but the crazed investments in its name are. Just make sure you are sitting in a chair when the music stops.
