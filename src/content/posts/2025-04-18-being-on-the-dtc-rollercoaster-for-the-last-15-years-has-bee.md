Being on the DTC rollercoaster for the last 15 years has been exciting, at the very least. It went from "this is amazing, this is the future" to "how are you actually going to be profitable" to "maybe this doesn't work" to "well, I guess we have to do wholesale."

As someone who has always been a true believer in direct-to-consumer, I have often bit my tongue when the conversation turns pessimistic around DTC. It wasn't that I didn't want to stand up for DTC, it just didn't seem worth the effort trying to explain it to people who either don't get it or really don't care about it. For me, DTC is a philosophy, not a business model.

The goal of DTC wasn't to start online only brands or cut out the middle man. It was to create a more honest and transparent relationship with the people who allow your business to exist, the customer. So for me DTC is not just a segment of omnichannel retail, it is the overriding philosophy of the business itself. It is asking yourself, your team, and the entire company: "What are we doing here, why are we doing any of this." If the answer doesn't involve making your customers' lives better, then it's not really DTC.

I am very happy to see large corporations like Levi Strauss & Company truly begin to embrace at least some of the DTC philosophy. Sure every business is complicated, with layer upon layer of bureaucracy, B2B relationships, and office politics. But all of that complexity and those machinations only exist because of the people that support the company and the brand, the customer. Without them, there is no company.
