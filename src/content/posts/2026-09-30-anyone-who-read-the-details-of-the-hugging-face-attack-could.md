Anyone who read the details of the Hugging Face attack could have easily predicted this was coming. OpenAI, in a race to pump out new models quickly, totally ignored basic security protocols.

This NY Times article is damning but certainly not surprising. OpenAI let their AI models and agent run wild despite strong internal objections. Reading through the independent review of the Hugging Face attack was like watching a horror movie.

As someone who has almost exclusively worked in startups, it is not that hard to see what happened here. ChatGPT 3 was launched with little notice. Not even a press release. People found it and were blown away. The exurbence in Dec 2022 was mostly about what ChatGPT could be, not what it was in that moment.

Just like that, OpenAI became a trillion-dollar company, in theory. Now they needed to grow as fast as possible to catch the wave. I have been in similar situations, albeit at a much smaller scale. I assume it's mostly the same friction. You are building the bridge as you cross it. There is no time for planning or looking ahead. It is just laying down bricks right before you step on them, hoping the whole thing doesn't fall apart.

The problem with this of course is that you create a tangled mess. The goal is to survive long to make enough money, gain enough customers, or secure enough market share for your next round. Then you hire someone to clean it all up. OpenAI, despite never wanting for cash, never allowed themselves to slow down. It was just sprint after sprint after sprint.

OpenAI launched on December 11, 2015, as a non-profit AI research organization, its original mission and company goal was to build safe Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) and ensure its benefits are distributed broadly to all of humanity. Reread that. Non-profit. Safe AI. Their entire mission was to help build safe AI. Now look at where they are at.

The reason I coined the term Feral AI is because people often blame AI for all these attacks and hacks. Some people even call it rogue AI. I think this is the wrong framing. It blames the AI for the attacks, not the human beings that created the AI and then released it without adequate guardrails into the world.

Make no mistake, the real danger of AI is human beings misusing it. Not the AI itself. OpenAI has more resources than pretty much any AI company in the world and they chose to be extremely negligent. Now they are facing the consequences.

It is a tale old as time in Silicon Valley. Great idea, brilliant execution. But the fame and popularity from what they had built has turned caustic. Greed, hubris, and a desire for power have decimated OpenAI. How else can you explain how a nonprofit created to make AI safe for the public is now responsible for unleashing dangerous automated agents of destruction onto the open internet? The tragedy is Shakespearean.
