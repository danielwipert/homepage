"Someone at a major payments processor spent over $100,000 in tokens in a single week" - The end of tokenmaxxing

For the last two years, a lot of enterprise AI adoption has been driven by a simple mandate: use more AI, run more prompts, automate more workflows, generate more outputs, and prove that the company is “AI First”

That era is officially over. The most striking anecdote from Ed Zitron’s recent AI cost critique is that someone at a major payments processor reportedly spent more than $100,000 in AI tokens in a single week, while the company only had a vague way of tracking the spend.

That is not an AI strategy; that is FOMO bubblespeak. Look, I get money is nebulous and corporate financing is a dark art. But we are now in La La Land with AI spending. The budgets make no sense. No one can back them up. And the rug pull has already happened.

The next phase of enterprise AI will not be about maximizing usage. It will be about governing usage. Companies will need to know what each workflow costs, what business outcome it improves, and whether the value created is greater than the tokens burned.

You know, like running a business. Children that run lemonade stands have more business acumen than half the people leading the AI charge right now.

“Use more AI” was the experiment. It failed. Now is the time to start building systems with Gen AI that can better your company and show an actual ROI.
