“Stories sell stocks in the short term, but financial fundamentals determine long-term value.”

In his The Globe and Mail OpEd, Sam Sivarajan compares today’s investors to sailors “surrounded by an ocean of information yet thirsting for wisdom.” The rise of AI has turned markets into echo chambers where stories beat substance.

Key takeaways:

- Over 60% of young U.S. investors now get their financial insights from social media.
- Companies that simply mention “AI” in announcements often see a short-term stock boost.
- Harvard research shows AI-generated investment content is lower quality but still influences trading.
- Professor Yuan Zou warns that “over time, all of these viewpoints will converge to one view — the bot view.”
- Nobel laureate Herbert Simon wrote that “a wealth of information creates a poverty of attention.”

Sivarajan’s advice for cutting through the noise:

- Focus on fundamentals, not narratives.
- Seek diverse perspectives and challenge consensus.
- Match your time horizon to your goals, not to market fads.
- Recognize your biases and protect against herd behavior.
