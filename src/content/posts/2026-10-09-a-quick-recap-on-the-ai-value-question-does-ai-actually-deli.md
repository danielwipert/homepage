A quick recap on the AI Value question: does AI actually deliver any objective value to a company? I have covered three major studies so far: two in software development and one in sales/cx.

In software development, the value add exists mostly at the task and productivity level. 10x increase in code produced. But the studies did not see releases go up anywhere near the same level. They are being bottlenecked by the code review process.

Perhaps it is not surprising that the one thing that is supposed to catch mistakes is holding back agentic AI in coding. That 10X increase in code led to only about a 20% increase in new releases. The verification layer is where this exponential rise in code production is suffocating. Now whether that is a good or bad thing is beyond my scope. We are looking for realized value, not potential value.

Sales and CX are showing something different. There we are seeing a very clear value add in actual sales due to AI. The value add in those channels is mostly focused on gaps in the sales funnel and cx support. The biggest jump  in sales directly attributed to AI was when a customer was receiving no support in the sales funnel. AI filled the gap and got some of them to convert. Here AI works best as augmentation, not replacement. But in contrast to coding, where value add is quarantined, AI can clearly add bottom-line value to sales and cx workflows.

Perhaps the 2nd elephant in the room is how much has been invested in AI vs the actual value add of AI. As of right now, the ledger is so deep red it almost seems black. Cue the scene in The Big Short, "It's a bubble." The reality is that the hype and investment in AI so far outstrips its actual value add that it makes the dot.com boom look quite quaint. How that plays out, I just don't know.

We saw how volatile the AI market is over the last 48 hours as OpenAI made 70 billion or 50 billion dollars, depending on how you define revenue. The market blinked, and we got a glimpse of the chaos to come. 200 billion dollars was wiped out due to miscommunication or accounting sophistry. That is perhaps the true conflict in AI right now.

AI will create value. But we don't know how much, and we absolutely don't know when. Instead of corporations heeding this uncertainty, they have poured hundreds of billions into a risky bet. Because in order for AI investment to pay off, you need to know both how much value it will add and when it will be added.

Similar to the internet revolution, the AI era will go through waves. We are in the show me phase, and it will not go well for heavily leveraged investors. But AI is not going away, just like pets.com didn't kill ecommerce. Progress will be slow, winding, and then all of a sudden.
