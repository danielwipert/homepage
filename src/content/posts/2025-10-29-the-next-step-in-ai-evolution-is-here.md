The Next Step in AI Evolution is Here

Researchers at the USC Viterbi School of Engineering have created artificial neurons that physically replicate the electrochemical behavior of real brain cells, a milestone that could dramatically reduce chip energy use and move us closer to artificial general intelligence.

“It’s not that our computers aren’t powerful enough. It’s that they aren’t efficient enough. They use too much energy.”

The new design uses a “diffusive memristor”, a device that mimics the motion of ions rather than electrons. Unlike existing silicon chips that only simulate neural activity, these new devices embody it. Each artificial neuron takes up the space of a single transistor, compared to the tens or hundreds required in current designs, meaning chip size and energy use can be reduced by orders of magnitude.

“We are designing the building blocks that will allow AI to perform with a similar level of intelligence, without burning energy we cannot sustain.”
