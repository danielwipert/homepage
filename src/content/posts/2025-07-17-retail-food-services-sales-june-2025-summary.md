Retail & Food Services Sales – June 2025 Summary

U.S. retail and food services sales rose 0.6% in June 2025 to $720.1 billion, following a revised 0.9% decline in May. Compared to June 2024, sales are up 3.9%, indicating modest year-over-year growth amid uneven sectoral performance. The consumer economy showed resilience in June, especially in services and online channels, even as categories like fuel and durable goods softened.

Good News  
  • Food services and drinking places rose 0.6% MoM and 6.6% YoY, underscoring continued consumer spending on dining out.

- Nonstore (online) retailers posted a 4.5% YoY gain, showing persistent strength in e-commerce.

- Health and personal care stores rose 0.5% MoM and a robust 8.3% YoY.

- Clothing and accessories stores improved 0.9% MoM and 3.9% YoY, showing modest recovery.

Bad News

- Gasoline station sales were flat MoM and down 4.4% YoY, continuing a multi-month slump due to lower fuel prices.

- Electronics and appliance stores declined 0.1% MoM and 0.2% YoY, reflecting cooling demand in that segment.

- Department stores fell 0.8% MoM and 3.6% YoY, extending their structural decline.

- Building materials and garden supplies were down 1.1% YoY, signaling softness in home improvement spending.

Mixed News

- Auto and parts dealers rose 1.2% MoM, recovering from a 3.8% drop in May, but volatility remains high.

- General merchandise stores posted a 0.5% MoM gain and 3.2% YoY growth—stable, but not accelerating.

- Furniture and home furnishings declined slightly MoM (-0.1%) but are still up 4.5% YoY.

Source: U.S. Census Bureau, Advance Monthly Retail Trade Survey (Release: July 17, 2025)
