I will push back on this HBR article. Yes there is a "last mile" problem with AI.

Sure, AI is so transformative that the organizational design and theory has not caught up. In short, we don't know how to use AI in the workplace yet. How do we manage a company of people and AI agents?

We definitely do need to shift from doing the daily work to systems design. I am all-in on this. The problem here is that this article feels like MBA consulting lingo, not practical advice. Oh a big tech change requires us to change how we work, wow, how insightful. Please send the $500/hr invoice for your "consulting."

They call for a AI-Native firm. What exactly is that? That is like calling a human being an "oxygen consumption organism." All business is AI now. We passed the rubicon Nov 2022. That game is long over. All business requires AI now, no exceptions. A plumber needs to be using AI.

This is also a very MBA top-down view. There is nothing wrong with viewing business from a theoretical and strategic viewpoint. But theory and strategy without direct experience are worthless. This article comes across as leadership fluff. The same type of fluff the major consulting firms would sell you for several thousand dollars.

AI is part of the daily workflow of every employee. AI is in the weeds. And you, as a business leader, need to get down in the dirt and get your hands dirty. If you don't, you will be left behind.

The real issue with AI is not at the organizational level. It is at the systems level. Not people systems, the actual software and app systems level. Gen AI has not passed any threshold of reliability and trust. That makes it nearly impossible to integrate with real business. The future is systems design. But not people systems, we actually have to build AI systems that we can trust before we incorporate them into our work.

If you want to better understand how to incorporate AI into the workplace,  go learn AI. Learn the basics of neural networks, deep learning, and large language models. You can't lead in the AI Era without that knowledge. This isn't a rearranging the deck chairs moment. This is a rebuild the ship moment.
